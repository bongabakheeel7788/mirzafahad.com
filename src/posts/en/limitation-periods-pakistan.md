Ask any district judge what kills more meritorious cases than bad evidence, and the answer is a statute most clients have never heard of: the Limitation Act. Rights in Pakistan carry expiry dates, courts enforce them with severity the public does not expect, and "I didn't know there was a deadline" is precisely the argument the Act exists to reject. This article is the clock-reading guide.

## The doctrine, briefly

Limitation law fixes periods for every species of claim; a suit filed beyond its period is dismissed as time-barred even if otherwise unanswerable, and the court examines limitation **on its own**, whether or not the defendant raises it. The policy is repose — evidence decays, positions settle — and its effect is a discipline this entire series keeps encountering: the value of a claim is a function of the calendar.

## The periods that decide real cases

Without drowning in the schedule, the clusters that matter to this series' readers: **money claims** — suits on ordinary debts and contracts run on a three-year order of time from the amount falling due or breach, with the summary-suit instruments (cheques, notes) on their own clocks from dishonour: the arithmetic behind every warning in our loan and invoice articles. **Property claims** — longer clocks (possession claims historically twelve years; declarations and cancellations shorter, typically running from *knowledge*), with the co-heir principles our inheritance articles explain — possession of one heir counting for all — moderating the harshness for omitted heirs, but *knowledge without action* remaining fatal everywhere. **Specific performance** — the agreement-to-sell suits of our property series carry their own tight period from the fixed date or refusal. **Pre-emption** — the talb regime's days-and-fortnight clockwork, strictest in the whole system. **Appeals and revisions** — thirty to ninety days as the general landscape, counted from decree or order with certified-copy time excluded — the deadline family that produces the most condonation litigation. **Executions** — even a decree, hard-won, has enforcement windows; a judgment left to sleep can die in its own bed.

Two humane wrinkles: **acknowledgment and part-payment** — a written acknowledgment of a debt, or payment against it, restarts the money clocks (why our recovery articles treasure the WhatsApp admission); and **disability, fraud and concealment** — time's start adjusts where the claimant was legally disabled or the wrong was hidden, with fraud clocks running from discovery, as every inheritance-fraud discussion in this series has leaned upon.

## Condonation: the narrow gate

For appeals and certain applications, the law permits **condonation of delay** on "sufficient cause" — explained day by day, with courts demanding real accounts (illness documented, counsel's fault particularised) and rejecting the generic. For **suits**, there is effectively no condonation: the period is the period. Plan accordingly: condonation is an ambulance, not a schedule.

## Living with the clocks

The operational rules, distilled: date every grievance's birth in writing the day it happens (the refusal letter, the dishonour memo, the discovery of the mutation); consult counsel within days, not seasons, because the first consultation's most important output is a **limitation diary**; and when a deadline nears with negotiations pending, file anyway — suits can be withdrawn or settled; expired limitation cannot be revived by the other side's apologies. Every negotiating party in Pakistan knows the calendar; assume the one stalling you does too.
