Most people walk into a family court expecting the slow-motion procedure of civil litigation — and family law in Pakistan was deliberately built to be the opposite. The Family Courts Act 1964 creates a separate court, strips away most of the Civil Procedure Code and the strict evidence law, consolidates claims, and puts the judge under a statutory clock. Knowing the machinery removes most of the fear, whichever side of the aisle you sit on.

## One court, a wide schedule

Family courts have exclusive jurisdiction over the matters in the Act's Schedule: dissolution of marriage (including khula), dower, maintenance, restitution of conjugal rights, custody and guardianship, visitation, dowry and personal property of the wife, jactitation of marriage — and the family court also tries offences under the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance. For the wife's suits, jurisdiction lies where she **resides**, which spares a woman who has returned to her parents in Tandlianwala from litigating in her husband's distant city.

## The consolidated suit

The single most practical feature: a wife may combine her claims — dissolution, dower, maintenance for herself and the children, recovery of dowry articles — in **one plaint**, and the court decides them together. One court fee regime (nominal in family matters), one set of evidence, one judgment. Husbands can likewise consolidate their cross-claims, such as restitution of conjugal rights, and courts frequently club the husband's and wife's rival suits for joint trial.

## The lifecycle of a family case

1. **Plaint and summons.** The defendant gets a date to file a written statement; family courts move faster on service than civil courts.
2. **Interim maintenance.** The court can — and in Punjab practice, does — fix interim maintenance for the wife and children at an early hearing. The sanction has teeth: a husband who defaults on interim maintenance can have his **defence struck off**, converting the case into a near one-sided affair.
3. **Pre-trial reconciliation.** The judge must attempt reconciliation. In a khula suit, if reconciliation fails here, the court can pass the dissolution decree at this stage itself — which is why khula cases now end in months, not years. The wife is ordinarily directed to return or forgo the dower as consideration for khula.
4. **Issues and evidence.** Contested claims proceed to evidence. Section 17 of the Act excludes the Qanun-e-Shahadat and most of the CPC: proceedings are meant to be simple, documents come in without technical wrangling, and the court can question parties directly.
5. **Post-trial reconciliation and judgment.** A second reconciliation effort precedes judgment. The Act directs disposal within **six months**; where proceedings drag, a party can seek directions — and the clock is a genuine argument against adjournment-seeking.
6. **Execution.** Maintenance decrees are enforced vigorously: arrears can be recovered as arrears of land revenue, and persistent defaulters face coercive measures including detention.

## Appearance rules, especially for women

Family courts are built around the reality that many litigants are women appearing against their own in-laws. Appearance through counsel or a close relative holding a power of attorney is routinely permitted; personal appearance is insisted upon mainly for reconciliation sessions and the party's own testimony. Courts in Punjab also increasingly entertain evidence of overseas parties through video link — important for the many Faisalabad-area families with a spouse abroad.

## Appeals

Section 14 governs: decrees of a family court go in appeal to the **district court** or the **High Court** depending on the case and the judge's rank, within the limitation the Act prescribes. Two carve-outs matter: no appeal lies against a decree for dissolution by **khula**, and interim orders are generally not separately appealable — the legislature's way of preventing the appeal-every-order culture of civil litigation from infecting family cases. What cannot be appealed can occasionally be attacked through the constitutional jurisdiction of the High Court, but only for jurisdictional defects, not mere dissatisfaction.

## Practical notes from the trenches

- **Documents decide maintenance**: the husband's payslips, business records or visible lifestyle fix the quantum; the wife should plead his means specifically, and the husband who understates income invites the court to estimate against him.
- **Dowry lists** prepared at marriage, with photographs and receipts, transform a dowry-articles claim from oath-against-oath into arithmetic.
- **Children's maintenance continues regardless** of the custody fight's outcome, and can be increased periodically.

Family litigation is unavoidably emotional, but procedurally it is the most streamlined track in the Pakistani system. A brief consultation with the nikahnama, any payment records and the list of dowry articles usually settles what to claim, where, and in how many suits — almost always fewer than families expect.
