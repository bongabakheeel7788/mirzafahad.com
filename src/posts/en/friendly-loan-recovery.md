Money lent in friendship is recovered in law — if at all. The friendly loan is the most under-documented transaction in Pakistani life, and the cases it produces are won or lost almost entirely on what evidence the lender happens to possess. Here is a realistic map of the options.

## First, the proof audit

Before strategy, inventory what exists: bank transfer records (the strongest spine of any claim); the borrower's **cheque** — even post-dated, even later dishonoured; a **promissory note, receipt or IOU**; WhatsApp messages and voice notes acknowledging the debt and promising payment (electronic evidence is admissible and increasingly decisive); witnesses to the lending or to admissions; and any partial repayments (which both evidence the loan and refresh limitation). A loan made in cash, with nothing written and no messages, is legally a gift the borrower can deny — which is why the first advice in this article is retroactive: *always move money through the bank, always get an acknowledgment.*

## Route one: the summary suit

Where you hold a **cheque, promissory note or bill of exchange**, Order XXXVII CPC provides the fast track: a summary suit in which the borrower has no automatic right to defend — he must apply for **leave to defend** within a fixed time, showing a substantial defence, failing which the suit is decreed. Courts grant leave grudgingly against clean instruments. This is the premier recovery route: comparatively quick, pressure-generating, and it converts your paper into a decree. Limitation runs from the instrument's date/dishonour, so do not sit on it.

## Route two: the ordinary recovery suit

Without an instrument, the claim proceeds as an ordinary suit for recovery based on the loan transaction — bank entries, messages, witnesses, admissions. Slower and more contestable, but regularly successful where the money trail is clear. Plead the transaction precisely (dates, amounts, mode); vagueness is where these suits die. **Limitation matters acutely**: recovery claims carry short limitation windows, extended by written acknowledgments or part-payments — another reason those WhatsApp admissions are gold.

## The criminal dimension — used honestly

Where the borrower gave a cheque that bounced, **section 489-F** PPC adds criminal pressure (see our dedicated article). Where the money was obtained by deception from the outset — a fake investment, a forged document — **cheating (section 420)** may genuinely apply. But an ordinary unpaid loan is *not* a crime, and FIRs manufactured to convert civil debts into criminal pressure are recognised and undone by courts — sometimes at cost to the complainant. The criminal law is a lever where its ingredients truly exist, not a collection agency.

## The quiet middle: notice and settlement

A formal **legal notice** through counsel — stating the loan, the evidence, the demand and the deadline — resolves a remarkable share of these matters. It signals seriousness, creates a documented admission opportunity (replies often concede the loan while pleading time), and costs little. Structured settlements — schedules, post-dated cheques for instalments (each future security), a written acknowledgment — recover money that litigation would spend years chasing.

## What not to do

Do not seize the borrower's property or vehicle "against the loan" — that is an offence, and it converts your claim into his complaint. Do not involve police informally for pressure; it backfires legally. Do not keep lending to protect earlier lending. And do not let embarrassment run out the limitation clock — the friendships these loans were meant to preserve rarely survive the default anyway, but your money can, if the file reaches a lawyer while the dates are still alive.
