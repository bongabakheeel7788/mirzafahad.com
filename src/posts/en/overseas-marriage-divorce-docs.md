For overseas Pakistanis, family status lives in two legal systems at once — and most of the misery in this field comes from an event valid in one system remaining invisible in the other: the marriage the embassy cannot verify, the divorce Pakistan never learned of, the spouse visa stuck on a certificate mismatch. The cure is understanding which paper proves what, where.

## Marriage: getting one record everywhere

**Nikah in Pakistan:** ensure registration with the **Union Council** and obtain the **NADRA computerised Marriage Registration Certificate (MRC)** — the document foreign authorities and embassies actually rely on. For use abroad it travels with an attestation chain: **MOFA attestation**, plus the destination country's requirements (embassy legalisation or, for Hague-convention destinations, apostille under Pakistan's accession).

**Marriage abroad:** a marriage validly performed abroad is recognised, but Pakistan's records won't reflect it automatically. Register through the **Pakistani mission** (missions perform/register nikahs) or have the foreign certificate channelled into NADRA's records so the family's Pakistani documentation (FRC, children's registration, inheritance) stays coherent. Update your **NADRA marital status** — mismatches between CNIC/NICOP data and claimed status generate exactly the embassy queries families dread.

**Children:** register births at the mission promptly (CRC/NICOP), keeping the chain — marriage record → child registration — unbroken; inheritance and nationality questions decades later trace back to these entries.

## Divorce: the double-registration trap

This is where lives get stuck. A husband in Pakistan or abroad pronouncing talaq must complete the **section 7 MFLO process** — written notice to the relevant Union Council (for overseas Pakistanis, the mission channels this role), the ninety-day arbitration period, and the **effectiveness certificate**, then NADRA's divorce registration. A foreign court divorce between Pakistani spouses similarly should be recognised into the Pakistani record — through the Union Council/NADRA process, with the foreign decree attested — rather than left as a foreign paper Pakistan has never seen.

The trap's shape: spouses divorced abroad but still married in NADRA's records (blocking remarriage documentation and inviting bigamy allegations), or "divorced" in Pakistan by an unnotified talaq that never legally completed (leaving maintenance and inheritance alive). **Khula from abroad** runs through the Pakistani Family Court — filed through counsel under a special POA, with the wife's evidence managed around appearances; the resulting decree then feeds the Union Council/NADRA chain like any other dissolution.

After completion, obtain the NADRA **Divorce Registration Certificate** and attest it for the foreign system — the mirror of the marriage chain.

## The spouse-visa file

Embassies triangulate: the nikahnama and NADRA MRC, both spouses' identity documents with consistent marital status, and — where prior marriages existed — the complete termination papers (divorce certificates, death certificates) on both sides. Assemble the file so every status change in either country has its certificate; the commonest refusals are documentary, not substantive: a name spelled three ways, an unregistered talaq, an MRC never attested.

## Practical rules

Register every event — marriage, divorce, birth — in **both** systems within months, not years; keep originals plus attested sets; align names and dates across CNIC/NICOP, passports and certificates (correct discrepancies through NADRA once, formally); and before remarrying anywhere, confirm the prior marriage's termination is complete in **Pakistani** records specifically. Families who maintain this discipline pass through embassies and inheritances unremarked; the alternative is explaining, years later and under suspicion, a paper trail that was always innocent — just incomplete.
