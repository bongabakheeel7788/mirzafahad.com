In every village fight and street brawl that reaches a police station, the medico-legal certificate soon fills with unfamiliar words — *shajjah-i-khafifah*, *jurh ghayr jaifah*, *itlaf-i-udw*. These are not medical jargon but legal classifications from the Pakistan Penal Code's hurt chapter, inserted by the Qisas and Diyat law, and each label carries its own punishment and its own compensation. Understanding the grid tells you what a case is really worth — as accused or as victim.

## The architecture of the hurt chapter

Sections 332 to 337 PPC replace the old simple/grievous hurt scheme with an Islamic-law grid. Section 332 defines hurt generally: causing pain, harm, disease, infirmity or injury, or impairing or dismembering an organ, without causing death. The chapter then branches:

- **Itlaf-i-udw (section 333)** — dismembering or severing any limb or organ.
- **Itlaf-i-salahiyyat-i-udw (section 334)** — destroying or permanently impairing the *functioning* of a limb or organ, or causing permanent disfigurement.
- **Shajjah (section 337-A)** — injuries to the head or face, graded in six steps from *khafifah* (no bone exposed) through *mudihah* (bone exposed), *hashimah* (bone fractured), *munaqqilah* (bone fractured and dislocated), up to *ammah* and *damighah* (reaching or rupturing the membrane of the brain).
- **Jurh (sections 337-B to 337-F)** — wounds on any other part of the body, principally divided into *jaifah* (reaching the body cavity) and *ghayr jaifah*, which itself grades from *damiyah* (skin broken, bleeding) up to *munaqqilah*.

The medico-legal officer's classification is therefore the single most consequential document in a hurt case: it selects the section, which selects the punishment and the compensation.

## Arsh: compensation fixed by law

For specified injuries, the Code itself fixes compensation — **arsh** — as a percentage of the value of **diyat**. Examples: a *hashimah* fracture of the skull carries a prescribed percentage; each tooth has its percentage; loss of both of a paired organ (both eyes, both hands) attracts the full diyat, while loss of one of a pair attracts half. The diyat value itself is notified by the Federal Government for each financial year, pegged to a prescribed quantity of silver, so the rupee figure rises over time and must always be checked for the year of the judgment.

Arsh is the **victim's money**, not a fine to the State. The Code backs it with teeth: the court can order payment in instalments, recover it as arrears of land revenue, and keep the convict in jail (as if under simple imprisonment) until arsh is paid or secured.

## Daman: compensation assessed by the court

Injuries outside the arsh schedule — most *shajjah-i-khafifah* cases, bruises, many *ghayr jaifah* wounds — are compensated by **daman**: an amount the court determines in its discretion, considering medical expenses, loss of earnings and the nature of the injury. Daman enjoys the same recovery machinery as arsh. In practice, realistic documentation — hospital bills, medicines, time off work — directly shapes the daman figure, so victims should preserve every receipt.

## Ta'zir on top

Arsh and daman are compensation; they do not exhaust the State's interest. Most hurt sections also provide imprisonment **as ta'zir** in addition to arsh or daman, and certain aggravated situations — hurt by dangerous means or weapons (section 337-H read with the grid), hurt to extort a confession, hurt on a public servant — carry enhanced ta'zir. So an accused may pay arsh and still serve a sentence.

## Compromise: where these cases usually end

Because the compensation right belongs to the victim, hurt offences are by and large **compoundable**. The injured person may waive (*afw*) or compound for agreed *badl-i-sulh* with the court's permission, and most fight cases in tehsil courts around Faisalabad end exactly this way — a written compromise, statements recorded, accused acquitted of the compoundable counts. Two cautions: compounding needs the genuine, un-coerced consent of the victim (and of all victims, where several were hurt), and the court retains discretion in aggravated forms to impose ta'zir despite compromise.

## What each side should do early

- **Victim**: get the medico-legal examination immediately — delay degrades the classification — and keep every treatment record for daman.
- **Accused**: scrutinise the MLC date, the doctor's classification and whether the injury described matches the weapon alleged; a *hashimah* downgraded to *khafifah* on re-examination transforms the case.

The hurt grid rewards precision on both sides. A brief consultation with the FIR and the medico-legal certificate in hand usually settles whether the road ahead is trial, compromise, or a challenge to the classification itself.
