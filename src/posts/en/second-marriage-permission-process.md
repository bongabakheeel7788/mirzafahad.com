The man who contracts a second nikah quietly, assuming religion alone governs the matter, discovers Pakistani law's position only when the first wife's lawyer does: a criminal complaint, a demand for the entire dower at once, and a dissolution suit. Section 6 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 does not prohibit polygamy — it regulates it through a permission process that is short, cheap, and far less painful than the consequences of skipping it.

## The legal rule

No man may contract a second marriage during the subsistence of an existing marriage **except with the previous permission in writing of the arbitration council** — the body convened by the Union Council chairman. A nikah registrar is not supposed to register a second marriage without that permission attached. The rule applies to every married Muslim man in Pakistan, and it applies even where the first wife privately consents: her consent is a factor, not a substitute for the permission.

## Step-by-step procedure

1. **Written application** to the chairman of the Union Council of the area, stating the **reasons for the proposed marriage** and whether the **consent of the existing wife (or wives) has been obtained**. The prescribed form and a modest fee apply; the fee changes, so check locally.
2. **Constitution of the arbitration council.** The chairman asks the husband and each existing wife to nominate a representative. The council is the chairman plus these representatives.
3. **Hearing.** The existing wife may appear, consent, or object. The council examines whether the proposed marriage is **just and necessary** — the statutory test. The rules framed under the Ordinance illustrate what may count: sterility, physical infirmity, physical unfitness for conjugal relations, wilful avoidance of a decree for restitution of conjugal rights, or insanity of the existing wife. None of these is automatic; the council weighs each case.
4. **Decision.** Permission is granted or refused in writing, and the council may attach conditions — commonly about maintenance and equal treatment.
5. **Revision.** Either side aggrieved by the grant or refusal may seek revision before the Collector (the designated revenue authority), whose decision the Ordinance makes final. In practice, writ petitions still reach the High Court against flagrantly defective proceedings.

The whole process is designed to take weeks, not years. Men who find it humiliating should weigh it against the alternative below.

## What happens without permission

The Ordinance stacks four consequences:

- **Immediate dower liability.** The husband must pay the existing wife her **entire dower, prompt and deferred, immediately**. A deferred mehr of land or gold that was supposed to wait for death or divorce falls due at once, recoverable through the family court.
- **Criminal liability.** On complaint, the husband is punishable with simple imprisonment and fine. Punjab enhanced these penalties by its 2015 amendment to the MFLO; the figures change, so verify the current ones. Prosecution in Punjab proceeds on the Union Council's or aggrieved party's complaint before the family court, which is empowered to try MFLO offences.
- **Ground for dissolution.** The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939 lets the first wife seek dissolution where the husband has taken another wife in contravention of the MFLO's provisions — and the modern khula route is open regardless.
- **Registration trouble.** The unregistered or irregularly registered second nikah creates documentary problems for the second wife and her children — NADRA records, inheritance, immigration — that take years to untangle, usually through court declarations.

Note what the law does **not** do: it does not invalidate the second nikah itself. The second wife and her children retain their status and inheritance rights; the punishment lands on the husband.

## Practical advice for each party

- **Husband:** apply honestly, state real reasons, and offer concrete maintenance arrangements — councils respond to demonstrated fairness. Never let a nikah registrar talk you into a quiet unregistered ceremony.
- **First wife:** you are entitled to nominate your representative, be heard, and demand your full dower the moment an unauthorised marriage occurs. Your remedies — dower suit, maintenance, dissolution, complaint — can run in parallel in one family court.
- **Prospective second wife:** insist on seeing the arbitration council's written permission before the nikah; your own future paperwork depends on it.

Every second-marriage situation sits at a different point between negotiation and litigation. A brief consultation with the nikahnama, the Union Council file and any notices exchanged usually settles which of these remedies — or defences — actually fits.
