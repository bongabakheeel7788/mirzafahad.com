The overseas Pakistani's property problem follows a script so standard that lawyers can narrate it before the client finishes: property bought or inherited at home, entrusted to a brother or uncle, years of remittances — and then the discovery, on a visit or at a death, that the record tells a different story. The losses are preventable, but only by systems set up before trust fails.

## The five standard patterns

**The widened power of attorney.** A POA given for "managing" the plot is used to sell it — to the attorney's own relative — or a general POA signed decades ago surfaces to justify transfers. (Our dedicated POA article covers the drafting and revocation safeguards.)

**The silent mutation.** An oral-gift or sale mutation appears in the revenue record while the owner is abroad; nobody sends notice to Toronto. Discovered years later, it has grown sub-sales and constructions on top.

**The benami purchase.** Property bought with the expatriate's money but recorded in a brother's name "for convenience" — and later claimed by him as his own. The benami transactions law and its litigation reality make recovery possible but evidence-hungry: the money trail is everything.

**The occupied house.** The family home lived in by relatives who, over time, reconceive themselves as owners — resisting sale, collecting rent, sometimes producing "agreements" from the deceased parents' era.

**The inheritance squeeze.** The overseas sibling's share managed around: mutations processed without them, or a relinquishment obtained during a bereavement visit.

## The protection system

**Paper your ownership independently.** Hold the original title documents abroad; obtain fresh **fards periodically** (PLRA's systems make remote and online checking realistic) — an annual record check is the single highest-value habit, because every scheme above needs time and silence to mature.

**Route money traceably.** Purchases and constructions funded through banking channels, in your name, with the deed in your name. If circumstances ever force another's name, contemporaneous documentation of the true ownership — declarations, the money trail, possession arrangements — decides the future benami dispute.

**Engage professionals, not only relatives.** A local lawyer on a standing brief for record checks and notices costs little; tenancy through formal agreements with rent to your bank account converts the "caretaker relative" ambiguity into documented landlord-tenant clarity. Where relatives do occupy, a written licence acknowledging your ownership — signed in good times — prevents the possession-into-ownership drift.

**Restrict and register authority.** Narrow, specific, registered POAs; immediate registered revocation when purposes end; and no signatures on Pakistani stamp papers during emotional visits without your own lawyer reading them first.

## When it has already happened

Speed is the entire game — every year of delay adds bona fide purchasers, constructions and evidentiary decay. The toolkit: **civil suits** for declaration/cancellation of the offending instruments and mutations, with urgent injunctions against further alienation; **Illegal Dispossession Act** complaints where possession was taken; criminal complaints for forgery and fraud where documents were fabricated; and the **Overseas Pakistanis' facilitation channels** — dedicated OPF/overseas commissions and, in Punjab, prioritized handling for overseas complaints — which can accelerate revenue-record corrections and police attention. Litigation from abroad is manageable: cases proceed through a special attorney (your own carefully-limited POA to counsel), with your evidence arranged for recorded appearances — plan testimony with counsel around visits or video-link facilities where available.

## The honest summary

Distance does not lose property; undocumented trust does. The expatriates who keep title papers, check records annually, move money through banks and formalise every family arrangement almost never appear in these cases. The system above costs a few days a year — against the standard alternative, which costs a decade in the courts of a country you no longer live in.
