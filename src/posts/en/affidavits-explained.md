No document is signed more casually in Pakistan than the affidavit — churned out by the courthouse photocopier, sworn in seconds, filed and forgotten. And no casual signature carries more delayed danger: affidavits are read years later, against changed circumstances, by opponents hunting contradictions. Understanding the instrument is basic legal self-defence.

## What an affidavit is

An affidavit is a **written statement of facts, sworn or affirmed before an authorised attesting officer** — an oath commissioner at the courts, a notary public, or designated officials — by a deponent who takes responsibility for its truth on oath. Its uses saturate the system: verification of pleadings, evidence in interlocutory matters, undertakings to courts and authorities, declarations for NADRA, banks, universities, societies, and the "general affidavits" every office demands.

Its power and its danger are one feature: **it is sworn testimony in written form**. A false affidavit engages the perjury and false-evidence provisions of the criminal law, and — more commonly consequential — a demonstrated false statement in one affidavit contaminates the deponent's credibility across an entire litigation. Cross-examiners collect a party's affidavits the way accountants collect receipts.

## The stamp paper mythology

Two clarifications this jurisdiction badly needs. First: **stamp paper is revenue, not magic** — it evidences payment of stamp duty where an instrument requires it; it does not convert falsehood to truth, an unattested letter into an affidavit, or an unenforceable arrangement into a contract. Second, the reverse: many perfectly binding documents need no stamp paper at all, while some documents on lavish stamp paper (the "stamp paper agreement" for transactions the law requires to be registered — property sales being the chronic example) remain legally inadequate despite the ceremony. Our property articles detail that particular trap.

## Signing discipline

Before your thumb or signature touches an affidavit: **read every line** — including the recitals a clerk "always includes"; verify facts stated as your knowledge actually are your knowledge (belief and information have their own proper phrasing — "to the best of my information and belief" — and the distinction matters in court); date accuracy, because affidavits are timeline evidence; and refuse blanks — an affidavit with unfilled fields is a blank cheque of testimony. For the routinely demanded "undertaking" affidavits — societies, employers, institutions — understand you are creating enforceable commitments, not paperwork: the undertaking not to claim, not to build, to vacate by a date, will be enforced as exactly that.

Attestation is not decoration: the deponent appears before the attesting officer, is identified, and swears — the officer's certificate says so. The market's shortcut, attestation without appearance, produces documents vulnerable to challenge precisely when they matter, and honest attestation protects the *deponent* too: it defeats later claims that the signature was obtained by fraud.

## Affidavits from abroad

Overseas deponents swear before **Pakistani missions** (or use the attestation chains our overseas-documentation article maps); affidavits so attested serve in Pakistani proceedings and offices, and the special-attorney affidavits that drive litigation from abroad follow the narrow-drafting rules our POA article insists on.

The single-line summary this document deserves: an affidavit is testimony you cannot cross-examine your way out of later — write it, read it, and swear it accordingly.
