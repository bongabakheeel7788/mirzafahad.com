Default panic produces two opposite mistakes: borrowers who vanish — guaranteeing the worst sequence — and borrowers who sign anything the recovery officer brings, guaranteeing the second-worst. The actual legal machinery is neither as savage as the recovery calls imply nor as forgiving as hope suggests, and knowing its real shape is what lets a distressed borrower steer.

## The sequence

**Demand and classification.** Missed instalments trigger reminders, then formal demand notices, while the account degrades through the State Bank's classification categories and enters the **credit information system** — the listing that quietly matters most, because it follows you to every bank for years.

**The banking court.** Banks sue under the financial institutions recovery framework in dedicated **banking courts** — a leave-to-defend regime like the summary procedure our invoice-recovery article describes: the borrower must obtain the court's leave to contest by disclosing a substantial defence, failing which decree follows on the bank's documents. Genuine defences exist — miscalculated markup, payments not credited, charges beyond the agreement — but "times are hard" is not among them; the forum is built for speed.

**Execution and security.** Decree in hand, the bank executes: **mortgaged property** is sold through the court's processes (with the framework giving financial institutions notably strong enforcement rights over secured assets), hypothecated stock and vehicles are seized, accounts attached. Unsecured loans — personal loans, credit cards — end in the same decree but hunt for attachable assets and salary.

**Guarantors** discover their signatures were real: liability is joint and several, and the guarantor's assets stand behind the borrower's default. Standing guarantor is lending your balance sheet — the theme our partnership and surety articles repeat.

## What is *not* in the sequence

Honest default is **not a crime**: no FIR lies for owing money, and recovery agents implying arrest are bluffing — note our companion articles on harassment limits and 489-F, because the exception is real: **security cheques** taken at disbursement become criminal leverage when bounced, and fraud in obtaining the loan (forged documents, fake collateral) is prosecutable. The wilful-defaulter machinery targets deliberate evasion with capacity to pay, a different animal from financial distress.

Recovery harassment has limits — calls to your workplace and relatives, abuse, threats — and the State Bank's conduct rules plus the Mohtasib route discipline banks that cross them. Document the calls; complaints work.

## The rescue window

Banks price recovery litigation realistically, which makes **restructuring and settlement** genuinely available to borrowers who engage early with numbers: tenor extensions, markup concessions, and in distressed accounts negotiated **settlements below book** against lump sums. The rules of that negotiation: engage in writing before decree (leverage shrinks at every stage), never promise what the next quarter cannot pay (a broken restructuring burns the last goodwill), and close every settlement with two documents — the bank's **full-and-final discharge** and its commitment to **correct your credit listing**. Distress without either paper is distress deferred.

Default is a legal process with exits at defined points, not a moral catastrophe with none. The borrowers who fare worst are those who stopped opening the bank's letters — because every letter unopened was an exit unexplored.
