The committee — the rotating pool that finances weddings, shop stock and motorcycle down-payments across Pakistan — runs entirely on trust, until the month it doesn't: the organiser's phone off, the early-ballot member who stopped paying, the app committee that evaporated. The law has answers, but they reward the participants who treated trust as documentable.

## What a committee is, legally

A private committee among colleagues or neighbours is an **informal mutual arrangement** — a web of loans each member makes to whoever takes each ballot — customary, tolerated, and unregulated. Legal significance attaches at two edges: **default**, where ordinary civil and criminal law applies to the unreturned money; and **scale**, where organisers collecting from the general public, advertising returns, or running committee "businesses" drift into the territory of unauthorized deposit-taking and the fraud frameworks — the graveyard of every collapsed app-committee scheme.

## The proof problem — solve it while trust is intact

Committee disputes are lost at collection time, not default time. The claims that succeed carry: **payment through traceable channels** (bank, wallet — even to an organiser who "prefers cash"); the **committee register or list** — members, amounts, ballot order — photographed at joining; the organiser's **acknowledgments in writing**, which WhatsApp provides effortlessly if payments are confirmed by message monthly; and, gold standard, **post-dated cheques or a signed acknowledgment** from whoever takes early ballots — the practice sophisticated committees already follow, converting each payout into documented debt owed back to the pool.

Members who pay cash, unrecorded, to an organiser they "know" hold a claim provable only by the community's memory — which scatters exactly when needed.

## When it breaks: the routes

**A member takes the early ballot and stops paying.** Civilly, the remaining members' claims are for the unpaid contributions — pursued as recovery (the summary route where cheques or notes exist, as our loan-recovery article details). The organiser who guaranteed the circle — many do, explicitly or by custom — answers on that undertaking.

**The organiser defaults or disappears.** Simultaneously civil (recovery of your paid contributions) and, where the facts show dishonesty from the outset or absconding with collections, **criminal** — cheating (420) and criminal breach of trust — pursued through FIR or, against influence, the complaint routes our FIR-refusal article maps. Speed matters identically here as in every fraud: assets dissipate, and the first claimants recover from what later claimants only litigate about.

**The app or "digital committee" collapse.** Preserve the app records immediately (screenshots, transaction histories — before the platform dies), and complain on the cybercrime and regulatory tracks alongside civil claims; these schemes' operators face the mass-fraud machinery, and coordinated complainant groups have moved cases that individuals could not.

## Running one safely

For organisers: keep the register, collect through traceable channels, take early-ballot security, and understand your role's exposure — custom makes you the circle's guarantor in most disputes. For members: know every participant or trust the organiser's guarantee in writing, never both absent; and treat the committee's total as money you have lent — because in law, that is precisely what it is.
