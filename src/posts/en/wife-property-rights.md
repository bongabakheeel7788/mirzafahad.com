A quiet legal fact sits beneath Pakistani family life: **marriage does not merge property**. There is no community of marital assets, no automatic half-share, no "joint" anything unless deliberately created. This cuts both ways — it protects everything a woman owns absolutely, and it denies her what many assume divorce would divide. Women who understand both halves arrange their affairs very differently.

## What is absolutely hers

**Dower (haq mehr)** — the marriage's own financial obligation to the wife: prompt dower payable on demand at any time, deferred dower a debt due on divorce or the husband's death, ranking against his estate. Property named as dower in the nikahnama's columns transfers real rights (our nikahnama article details the columns that matter).

**Dowry and bridal gifts** — articles and gold given to the bride from either side are her property under the 1976 Act, recoverable in specie or value through the Family Court (our dowry-recovery article covers the proof and valuation rules, including gold at current rates).

**Her earnings and acquisitions** — salary, business income, savings and what they purchase are hers alone; a husband has no legal claim over his wife's income, and her spending it on the household creates moral credit, not his ownership.

**Property in her name** — bought, gifted or inherited — is hers with full powers of management and disposal. Two protections follow: family pressure to sign it over engages the same fiduciary-suspicion doctrines our gift and inheritance articles describe (transactions benefitting those who control a woman's affairs must be proved genuine by the beneficiary); and her **inheritance shares** — as daughter, wife, mother — are the fixed entitlements that no family arrangement lawfully removes.

## What is *not* hers automatically

The symmetrical truth: assets in the **husband's** name — the house built during marriage, the business grown across it — are his in law, regardless of the domestic partnership behind them. Divorce divides nothing; her claims on exit are dower, iddat maintenance, children's maintenance, and dowry recovery. Pakistani law has no matrimonial-property regime — a reality that surprises many and shapes the advice below.

**The benami middle ground**: where property stands in her name but his money paid — or vice versa — the benami and contribution doctrines decide by money trail and intention. A wife whose funds or gold financed an asset in his name has a claim *if she can prove the contribution* — bank transfers, the jeweller's sale receipt, contemporaneous writing.

## Arranging affairs wisely

At marriage: set dower deliberately (it is the wife's financial floor, not a formality), record dowry and gold with the signed list our dowry article recommends, and use nikahnama column 17 for agreed financial terms. During marriage: keep her property and accounts in her name; document significant contributions to family assets at the time (a one-line acknowledgment signed when her gold sold to build the house's second floor outweighs years of later testimony); and treat pressure to "transfer for tax/ease/family" with the caution this series applies to all such transfers. At inheritance events: claim her share when it opens — the omitted sister of our women's-inheritance article is very often a wife told her share stayed "with the family".

None of this is preparation for divorce; it is the ordinary hygiene of ownership in a legal system that, for better and worse, keeps every marriage two estates.
