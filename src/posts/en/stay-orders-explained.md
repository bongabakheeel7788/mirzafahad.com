"He has taken a stay" may be the most feared sentence in Pakistani property dealings. The fear is often out of proportion to the law: a stay order is a provisional, defeasible protection — powerful when rightly obtained, and vulnerable when it isn't. Here is how these orders actually work.

## What a stay order is

A "stay" is a **temporary injunction** under Order XXXIX of the Civil Procedure Code: an order restraining a party, during a pending suit, from particular acts — alienating property, raising construction, dispossessing the plaintiff, blocking a transfer. It preserves the subject matter so the eventual decree is not rendered meaningless. It decides nothing about final rights.

## The three ingredients

Courts grant temporary injunctions only where the applicant shows all three: a **prima facie case** (a serious, arguable claim shown by documents, not just assertions); **irreparable loss** if the order is refused (loss that money cannot later fix — dispossession, demolition, alienation to third parties); and **balance of convenience** (the applicant suffers more from refusal than the respondent from grant). Weakness in any one is the standard ground on which stays are refused — or later vacated.

An ex parte stay (granted without hearing the other side) is meant for genuine urgency and carries obligations: the law expects prompt notice to the other side, and applications for temporary injunctions are meant to be decided within a defined short period rather than lingering.

## How long a stay really lasts

An interim stay lasts until the injunction application is decided, and a confirmed injunction typically lasts through the suit — but "through the suit" is not a life sentence: the respondent can have it **vacated** at any stage by showing the ingredients fail, circumstances changed, or the applicant abused it. Appellate courts also entertain challenges against injunction orders. The folk belief that "a stay means ten years" reflects litigation delay generally, not any property of the stay itself — a contested stay attacked promptly and competently often falls within months.

## Misuse — and the court's answers

The manufactured stay — a weak suit filed to freeze a genuine owner's sale or construction, seeking nuisance settlement — is a recognised pathology. The answers available: contest the injunction application immediately with the title record (prima facie case collapses against clean documents); seek **security/undertakings** — courts can condition stays on the applicant securing the respondent's losses; press for the statutory timeline on deciding the application; invoke **costs and compensation** provisions for frivolous injunctions; and where a party violates a stay, enforcement is equally real — disobedience invites attachment and detention under the CPC's enforcement provisions.

For the respondent, the single most effective step is unglamorous: **file the written reply to the injunction application fast, with documents**. Stays survive on the applicant's uncontradicted story; they die on the record.

## Stays beyond property

The same principles run through the wider family of interim relief: stays of recovery in banking suits, status-quo orders in partnership disputes, stays of dispossession in tenancy matters, and constitutional-jurisdiction interim orders against authorities (where separate statutory limits on duration and undertakings apply). In every form, the constant is the trio of ingredients and the provisional nature of the order.

## Practical guidance

If you need a stay: come with documents, come urgently (delay defeats "urgency"), ask precisely for what preserves the status quo, and comply scrupulously with any conditions — a stay-holder in breach loses the court's sympathy permanently. If you face one: do not despair and do not pay for its removal; read the plaint, attack the weakest ingredient, and force the application to decision. Stay orders reward preparation on the way in — and expose its absence on the way out.
