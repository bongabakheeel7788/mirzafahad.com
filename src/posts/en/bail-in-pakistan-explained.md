When an FIR is registered, the first practical question for the accused and his family is almost always the same: *will he be arrested, and can we get bail?* This article explains how bail works under the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 (Cr.P.C.) as it applies in Punjab, in plain terms.

## What bail is — and is not

Bail is release from custody, on the condition that the accused will attend the trial. It is **not** an acquittal and it is not a finding that the accused is innocent. The case continues. Bail simply means the accused faces it from home rather than from jail.

## Bailable and non-bailable offences

Every offence in the Pakistan Penal Code is classified in the Second Schedule of the Cr.P.C. as either bailable or non-bailable.

- In a **bailable** offence (for example, simple hurt under section 337-A(i), or criminal intimidation under section 506), bail is a right. The police or the court must release the accused on furnishing surety. Section 496 Cr.P.C.
- In a **non-bailable** offence (for example, theft under section 380, attempted murder under section 324, or cheque dishonour under section 489-F), bail is at the discretion of the court. Section 497 Cr.P.C.

"Non-bailable" does not mean bail is unavailable. It means the court decides.

## Post-arrest bail (section 497)

If the accused has already been arrested, the application is for post-arrest bail. The court considers, broadly:

1. **The prohibitory clause.** Section 497(1) says that where there are *reasonable grounds* to believe the accused is guilty of an offence punishable with death, life imprisonment or ten years' imprisonment, bail is not ordinarily granted. Below that threshold, bail is the rule and refusal the exception.
2. **Further inquiry.** Even in serious offences, if the evidence at this stage leaves the accused's guilt open to *further inquiry* — a delayed FIR, no direct evidence, a contradiction between the FIR and the medical report, an ongoing dispute suggesting false implication — section 497(2) allows bail.
3. **Statutory grounds.** A woman, a person under sixteen, a sick or infirm person, or an accused whose trial has not concluded within the statutory period may be entitled to bail on those grounds alone.
4. **Role and evidence.** The specific role attributed to the accused in the FIR, whether he is named or unknown, whether recoveries have been made, and whether co-accused with similar roles have already been bailed.

## Pre-arrest bail (section 498)

If the accused has not yet been arrested, he may apply for *pre-arrest* (anticipatory) bail. This is treated as an extraordinary remedy. The applicant must show, in addition to the ordinary considerations, that the arrest is motivated by **mala fide** — that the FIR was registered for ulterior reasons such as a property dispute, personal enmity or to pressure him in a civil matter.

The procedure is that the accused appears before the Sessions Court and is granted *ad interim* (temporary) pre-arrest bail, with notice to the prosecution. On the next date, after hearing the police and the complainant, the court either confirms the bail or recalls it, in which case the accused is arrested from the courtroom.

Because of that last possibility, a pre-arrest bail application should never be filed casually. The case must be prepared before the first appearance.

## Where to apply

- For most offences the first application goes to the **Court of Sessions** (Additional Sessions Judge) of the district where the FIR is registered. Some lesser offences go to the Magistrate first.
- If the Sessions Court refuses, a fresh application lies to the **Lahore High Court**.
- If the High Court refuses, the Supreme Court may be approached.

Each court considers the application afresh; it is not an appeal from the lower court's order.

## What happens at the hearing

The defence counsel argues the application; the prosecutor and the complainant's counsel oppose it. The court reads the FIR, the police report and any medical or recovery evidence. Bail hearings are usually short — the decision is made on the record, not on witnesses. If bail is granted, the accused furnishes **surety bonds** — a person of standing with property who undertakes that the accused will appear — in the amount the court fixes.

## Cancellation of bail

Bail once granted can be cancelled if the accused tampers with evidence, threatens witnesses, absconds or commits a fresh offence. It can also be cancelled if the order granting it was patently wrong. Compliance with the conditions of bail is essential.

## Practical advice

- Move quickly. In a non-bailable case, the first application should be prepared the day the FIR is known.
- Bring the FIR copy, the medical report if any, and any documents showing an existing dispute with the complainant.
- Arrange sureties in advance. A grant of bail is useless if no surety can be furnished that day.
- Do not contact the complainant or witnesses. It is the most common reason bail is cancelled.

Every bail application turns on the specific FIR and evidence. A lawyer who has read the FIR can usually tell you within minutes which court to approach and what the realistic prospects are.
