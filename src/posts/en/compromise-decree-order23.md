Most civil suits in Punjab do not end with a judgment after full trial; they end in a deal — brokered by elders, exhaustion or arithmetic. The law not only permits this, it provides machinery to convert a private deal into a court decree. Done properly under **Order XXIII CPC**, a compromise ends the war permanently. Done sloppily — a stamp-paper "raazinama" never placed on the record — it merely schedules the next war.

## What Order XXIII allows

**Order XXIII Rule 3 CPC** provides that where a suit is adjusted wholly or partly by any **lawful agreement or compromise**, the court shall record it and pass a **decree in accordance with it**, so far as it relates to the suit. Three words carry the weight:

- **Lawful**: a compromise cannot decree what the law forbids — it cannot validate a transaction the law voids, defeat third-party rights, or bypass mandatory statutes. The court is not a rubber stamp for any bargain.
- **Recorded**: the terms go on the court record. Standard Punjab practice is a written compromise deed signed by the parties, verified by their **statements recorded in court**, often on oath. That verification is your insurance — a party who confirmed the deal before the judge cannot easily disown it later.
- **Decree in accordance with it**: the decree follows the terms, so the terms must be drafted like a decree — precise amounts, dates, khasra numbers, possession timelines — because this exact text will one day be executed.

A compromise can also cover matters **beyond the suit** (common in family settlements bundling several disputes); the decree operates on the suit's subject-matter, while the wider agreement binds as a contract.

## The consent decree's special strength

A compromise decree is fully **executable** like any contested decree — attachment, possession, the whole Order XXI toolbox. And it enjoys a shield: **section 96(3) CPC** bars an appeal from a decree passed with the consent of parties. The theory is simple — you cannot consent in court on Monday and appeal on Tuesday. This finality is exactly why parties choose consent decrees over bare agreements: a private raazinama breached means a fresh suit; a consent decree breached means straight execution.

## When a compromise decree can be attacked

Narrowly, but genuinely. The recognised grounds track the defects that poison consent itself:

1. **Fraud or misrepresentation** — a thumb impression obtained on papers misread to an illiterate party, terms switched between the deal and the deed, concealment that induced the consent.
2. **No consent in fact** — counsel compromised without authority, or a party never appeared and the signatures are disputed.
3. **Incapacity** — a minor's or person-under-disability's interests compromised without the court's leave required by Order XXXII.
4. **Want of jurisdiction** in the court that passed the decree.

The procedural route matters: since the CPC amendments, the settled path in Pakistan is an application under **section 12(2) CPC** to the court that passed the decree — not a separate suit, which stands barred for challenges based on fraud and misrepresentation going to the decree's validity. Timing matters too: these applications are weighed with limitation and conduct in mind, and a party who enjoyed the compromise's benefits for years attacks it uphill.

## Withdrawal is not compromise

**Order XXIII Rule 1** lets a plaintiff withdraw a suit — but withdrawal **without permission to file afresh** bars a new suit on the same cause of action. Parties sometimes "settle" by the plaintiff simply withdrawing on oral assurances; when the assurance evaporates, so has the remedy. If the deal involves future performance, never withdraw — compromise on recorded terms, so breach leads to execution, not to a barred claim.

## Drafting the deal that holds

A durable compromise deed: identifies all parties and the suit; states consideration and mutual obligations with dates; deals expressly with possession, mutation and encumbrances in property matters; covers costs; and declares that the suit stands decided in these terms. Both parties then appear and have their statements recorded. Where land is involved, follow through on **mutation** immediately — a decree unimplemented in the revenue record breeds the next generation of litigation.

If a settlement is on the table in your pending case — or a compromise decree is being used against you that you never truly consented to — a brief consultation with the suit file and the draft or deed almost always settles whether to sign, how to redraft, or how to attack.
