Most people in Punjab swallow bad purchases — the mobile that died in a month, the workshop that wrecked the engine, the marriage hall that vanished with the booking — because they assume the legal route means years and lawyers' fees. Consumer courts were built to break that assumption, and used correctly they are among the fastest forums in the province.

## The framework

The Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005 establishes district **consumer courts** for claims by consumers against suppliers of defective **products** and providers of faulty **services**. "Consumer" means someone who buys for consideration for personal use — not for resale or commercial purposes — and "services" reaches banks, workshops, hospitals in their service dimension, couriers, educational service providers and beyond. Claims must generally be brought within the limitation the Act sets, counted from the purchase or from when the defect emerged, so delay is dangerous here as everywhere.

## The mandatory first step: the 15-day notice

The Act's gateway: before filing, serve the supplier a **written notice** — describing the purchase, the defect or fault, and the remedy demanded — and give **15 days** for a response. Send it by a provable mode (registered post AD, courier with receipt) and keep the proof; complaints filed without the notice fail at the threshold.

Treat the notice as a genuine settlement instrument, not a formality. A precisely drafted notice, itemising the defect with evidence attached, tells a rational trader that the cheap exit is compliance — and a meaningful share of matters end with a refund inside the fifteen days.

## Filing and process

The complaint is filed in the consumer court of the district of purchase or the supplier's business — on plain paper in prescribed form, with modest cost, **with or without a lawyer**. Attach everything: the receipt or proof of purchase (bank record, delivery message — absence of a formal receipt is not fatal but proof of the transaction is needed), the warranty card, photographs of the defect, expert or workshop reports where they exist, and your notice with its delivery proof.

Process is deliberately compressed: notice to the defendant, reply, evidence, decision — the Act directs expeditious disposal, and straightforward product cases genuinely conclude in months. The court can order **repair, replacement or refund**, award **damages for loss** caused by the defect (the ruined clothes from the faulty machine, the alternative transport during the dead car's saga), and impose consequences on traders for violations of the Act's duties, which include honest description, price display and receipt issuance.

## What wins and what fails

Winning complaints share a shape: a documented transaction, a specific defect stated in the notice, and a paper trail of the supplier's evasions. Failing complaints share one too: commercial purchases dressed as consumer ones, expired limitation, no notice, or a demand so inflated (crores for a burnt toaster) that the complaint reads as pressure rather than grievance. Claim your actual loss with evidence and the forum treats you seriously.

## Where it overlaps other remedies

Defective bank services also have the Banking Ombudsman; school fee disputes have their own regulatory layer; medical negligence has the healthcare commission (each covered in companion articles). The consumer court remains the general-purpose weapon — and the mere existence of a genuine consumer complaint, properly noticed, converts a dismissive shopkeeper into a negotiator more reliably than any amount of counter-argument at the counter.
