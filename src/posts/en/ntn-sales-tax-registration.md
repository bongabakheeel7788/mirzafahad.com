Tax registration questions reach a lawyer's desk in two forms: the new business asking what it must do, and the established one asking how bad the notice it just received is. This article addresses the first — because everything about the second is worse and costlier.

## The NTN: the baseline identity

The National Tax Number is a business's (and individual's) identity with FBR — for individuals, effectively the CNIC once registered through FBR's IRIS portal. Registration is free and quick, and it is the gateway to everything: filing returns, appearing on the **Active Taxpayers List (ATL)**, banking relationships, supplying to companies and government, import/export, and commercial utility connections. Sole proprietors register themselves with the business name; partnerships and companies register the entity. There is no meaningful category of business that functions well without one — including those below tax thresholds, since registration and *owing tax* are different things.

## Filer vs non-filer: the cost of staying out

Pakistan's system taxes the unregistered through withholding: **non-filers pay elevated advance/withholding tax rates** across daily economic life — property transactions (dramatically), vehicle purchase and token, banking transactions in various modes, dividends and profit on savings — and face escalating restrictions that policy keeps tightening on major purchases. For anyone with assets or transactions, non-filer status is not avoidance but a voluntary surcharge. Becoming a filer means registering and **filing the annual return** (and staying on the ATL); for most traders the annual compliance cost is a fraction of a single property deal's non-filer penalty.

## Sales tax: who must register

**Goods (federal — FBR):** manufacturers, importers, wholesalers and retailers within the net must register for sales tax; retailers are tiered, with larger (**Tier-1**) retailers — by size, utility consumption, chain status and similar criteria — subject to full registration and point-of-sale integration, while smaller retailers are handled through fixed regimes. Manufacturers and importers effectively cannot operate outside the net; traders' thresholds and schemes shift with each Finance Act, which is precisely why a business at the margins should have its position checked annually rather than assumed.

**Services (provincial — PRA in Punjab):** services are taxed provincially; restaurants, contractors, professionals, IT and dozens of listed service categories register with the Punjab Revenue Authority and charge/deposit services sales tax. A restaurant or service business "waiting to be found" is accumulating exactly the liability it fears.

Registered persons file monthly returns, charge tax on invoices, and claim input adjustments — the system rewards documented supply chains and punishes cash ones, since undocumented purchases lose input credit.

## What actually happens to the unregistered

Not dawn raids — data. Bank information, utility consumption, property and vehicle records, POS and supplier data flow into FBR's systems, generating notices: compulsory registration, best-judgment assessments of past years, penalties and default surcharge — arriving after several years have accumulated, at the moment least convenient. Sales tax non-registration adds its own penalties and, for those who charged tax without depositing it, genuinely serious exposure. The pattern across enforcement drives is consistent: those who register voluntarily negotiate their past comfortably; those compulsorily registered pay for the years the data reconstructs.

## The starter compliance stack

Register the NTN now; file the return annually and stay on the ATL; map your sales-tax position (FBR for goods, PRA for services) with a professional once, and re-check each budget; issue proper invoices and bank your revenue — documentation is also what makes the business sellable and bankable later. Total setup effort: days. The alternative's cost: assessed for you, years later, with surcharge.
