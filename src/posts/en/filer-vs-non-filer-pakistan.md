For years, staying out of the tax net was the comfortable default for traders, landlords and professionals across Pakistan. The state's response was not better enforcement but a price list: almost every significant transaction now costs dramatically more if your name is missing from one document — the Active Taxpayers List. Understanding how that list works is now basic financial literacy.

## The ATL: what "filer" legally means

"Filer" is shorthand for a person appearing on the **Active Taxpayers List (ATL)** that FBR publishes under the **Income Tax Ordinance 2001**. You get on it by **filing your income tax return** for the relevant tax year. The list is updated through the year; file after the deadline and you can still be added after paying a **surcharge under section 182A** — the "ATL surcharge" — at the amount currently prescribed for individuals, AOPs and companies.

The **Finance Act 2024** added a third status for certain transactions: the **late filer** — someone who filed, but after the due date — who pays intermediate withholding rates on property transactions, above filer rates but below non-filer rates. The policy message is blunt: file, and file on time.

Anyone can verify status in seconds through FBR's online ATL check or SMS service using CNIC — do this before any big transaction, because withholding agents will.

## Where non-filers bleed

The Ordinance's **Tenth Schedule** mechanically inflates withholding rates for persons not on the ATL — typically to double the filer rate, and in several cases far beyond. The pressure points:

- **Property.** Advance tax on purchase (section 236K) and sale (236C) runs at multiples of the filer rate for non-filers. Recent Finance Acts have gone further, moving toward restricting non-filers from purchasing higher-value immovable property and vehicles at all, subject to the current year's thresholds and conditions — check the position before committing to a deal.
- **Vehicles.** Advance tax at registration, transfer and annual token tax is sharply higher for non-filers, scaling with engine capacity.
- **Banking and savings.** Higher withholding on profit on debt for non-filers; cash withdrawal withholding applies to non-filers above a daily threshold under section 231AB.
- **Dividends, prizes and more.** The doubled-rate logic runs through dividends, prize bond winnings and a long list of other withholding heads.

Two structural points sharpen the pain. First, non-filer withholding is often economically **a tax on gross transactions, not income** — you pay it whether or not you made a rupee of profit. Second, while much of it is adjustable *if* you file a return, a non-filer by definition is not filing — so the money is simply gone.

## The myths

- **"I don't earn enough to file."** Filing and owing are different things. A return showing income below the taxable threshold is still a return, still gets you on the ATL, and still kills the penal withholding.
- **"Becoming a filer invites FBR attention."** The modern reality is reversed: FBR's data net — property registries, vehicle records, bank reporting, withholding statements — already sees non-filers' transactions, and non-filers are the enforcement priority.
- **"My property is in my wife's name, so no issue."** The spouse then needs filer status for the transaction rates, and unexplained assets in a non-earning spouse's name create their own problems under the Ordinance's unexplained-income provisions and the benami law covered elsewhere in this series.

## Becoming a filer

The sequence is short:

1. **Register on IRIS**, FBR's online portal — for individuals the CNIC becomes the NTN; registration needs a mobile number and email in your own name.
2. **File the return** for the latest complete tax year, with the wealth statement where required.
3. **If the deadline has passed**, pay the ATL surcharge with the return to enter the list, understanding the late-filer rate consequence for property transactions in the current regime.

For a salaried person or small trader the first return is genuinely modest work; the difficulty is almost always reconstructing the asset position for the first wealth statement honestly, so that future years reconcile.

Rates, thresholds and the restriction rules shift with every Finance Act — treat specific percentages you read anywhere as provisional. Before a property transfer, vehicle purchase or any six-figure withholding event, a brief consultation with your CNIC, last return (if any) and the transaction documents will establish your status, the exact current cost of it, and the fastest route to the cheaper column.
