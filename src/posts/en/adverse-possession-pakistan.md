Overseas Pakistanis ask this question more than almost any other: the family land has been in a cousin's or neighbour's hands for fifteen years — has it become his? Village occupiers ask the mirror question with hope. The legal answer has shifted over the last two decades, and it has shifted hard against the occupier.

## Where the 12-year figure comes from

The **Limitation Act 1908** fixes periods for suits. For possession of immovable property, the owner's suit must generally be brought within **12 years** of the possession becoming adverse. Section 28 adds the sting: when the period for a suit for possession expires, the owner's **right itself is extinguished**, not merely the remedy. Read together, these provisions built the classical doctrine: possess hostilely for 12 years, defeat the owner's suit, and keep the land.

That is the doctrine's skeleton. Its application in Pakistan today is another story.

## What the courts now say

The Supreme Court's modern jurisprudence treats adverse possession as a disfavoured, almost discredited plea. The reasoning recurs across judgments: the doctrine rewards a trespasser for the persistence of his wrong, sits uneasily with constitutional protection of property, and offends the Islamic principle that usurped property remains usurped — lapse of time does not launder a qabza. Courts therefore:

- construe the plea **strictly against the claimant**;
- require the occupier to plead and prove the **exact date** possession turned adverse, and every element of hostility;
- refuse to let a defendant blow hot and cold — claiming ownership by purchase *and* adverse possession in the alternative usually destroys both pleas;
- treat revenue entries showing the true owner as presumptive evidence the possession was never hostile.

An occupier hoping to convert sitting into owning faces, in practice, one of the steepest evidentiary climbs in civil litigation.

## What "adverse" actually requires

Not all long possession is adverse. The claimant must establish possession that is:

1. **Actual and continuous** — physical, unbroken occupation for the full period, not intermittent grazing or seasonal use.
2. **Open and notorious** — visible to the world and to the owner, not concealed.
3. **Hostile** — under a claim of right *against* the true owner, not under or through him.
4. **To the owner's knowledge** — the owner knew or plainly must have known, and slept on his rights anyway.

The categories that can never qualify, no matter the decades: **tenants** (possession is under the owner), **licensees and caretakers**, **co-sharers** (a co-owner's possession is presumed on behalf of all until a proven, communicated ouster), **relatives occupying by family arrangement**, and **mortgagees in possession**. Most village "possession since my father's time" stories fall in exactly these permissive categories — which is why they fail.

## Protecting your land, especially from abroad

For owners, the doctrine's residual danger is real only if you sleep for over a decade. The protection list is short and practical:

- **Paper the relationship.** If someone occupies with your permission — relative, tenant, caretaker — record it in writing, however simply, and refresh it periodically. Permissive possession never ripens.
- **Watch the fard.** Check the revenue record every year or two for hostile mutations or girdawari entries showing the occupier as cultivator in his own right; challenge wrong entries immediately before the revenue officer.
- **Interrupt adversity formally.** A legal notice asserting title, a suit for possession, or the occupier's own acknowledgment of your title (rent paid, a written admission) resets the clock or negates hostility.
- **Sue inside the period.** If possession has truly turned hostile, the suit for possession must be filed well within 12 years of that date. Waiting for a convenient visit home has cost families their land.

## If you are the occupier

Be honest about your category. If you entered permissively, the law offers you nothing by mere time — your route, if any, lies in proving an actual purchase, gift or family settlement. If you genuinely hold an old hostile possession, know that you carry the burden, the courts' scepticism, and the need for documentary proof of when and how your possession became adverse.

Every one of these disputes turns on dates, entries and the character of the original entry into possession. A brief consultation with the fard, girdawari record and any correspondence in hand will show quickly whether a claim or a defence is real — and what to file before time does its quiet work.
