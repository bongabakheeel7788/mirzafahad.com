When a parent dies in Pakistan and some heirs live abroad, geography reshapes the succession: the overseas heir depends on others for information, documents and representation — and the domestic relatives control the ground. Most overseas heirs are treated fairly; the structure below is for making fairness independent of luck.

## First moves from abroad

Within weeks of the death, from wherever you are: obtain the **death certificate** and NADRA **Family Registration Certificate** (establishing the heir list — verify you appear correctly; records gaps are fixable but fix them now); build the **asset map** — fards for land (obtainable through PLRA channels and counsel), bank accounts, business interests, files in societies; and engage **your own lawyer** — not the family's shared one. The heirs' interests diverge more than funerals suggest, and the overseas heir relying on the resident brother's lawyer has no independent eyes.

## The succession machinery, remotely

**NADRA succession certificates** are engineered for diaspora cases: heirs abroad complete **biometric verification at NADRA's overseas facilities/embassy arrangements**, and the certificate proceeds without travel where all heirs cooperate. **Court proceedings** (contested cases, letters of administration) accommodate absence through a **special power of attorney** to counsel or a trusted representative — executed before the Pakistani mission, narrowly drafted for the specific proceedings. Inheritance **mutations** for land likewise proceed with attested instruments and counsel's follow-through at the tehsil.

Draft every POA on the principle covered in our POA guide: specific proceedings, specific properties, express exclusion of authority to sell or compromise your share without written instructions, and expiry.

## Protecting the share you cannot watch

The vulnerabilities of absence have names: mutations recorded without you; **relinquishments** obtained during grief visits ("sign this to complete the paperwork" — never sign undated, unread, or unadvised documents in Pakistan, period); occupation of the family property hardening into ownership claims; and estate income (rents, crops, business profits) flowing to resident heirs indefinitely under "we're managing it". The counters: your lawyer's caveat-style vigilance on the record, written objections filed early, a demand for **accounts of estate income** (co-heirs in receipt owe the others their shares — mesne-profits logic applies), and moving the estate to formal division rather than indefinite management.

## Partition or sale of your share

An overseas heir wanting value out has three honest routes: **family settlement** — a registered deed dividing properties or buying out your share at documented valuation, with payment through banking channels before signing (the gold standard when relations are decent); **partition proceedings** through counsel where agreement fails; or **sale of your undivided share** to a third party — lawful, but discounted in the market and guaranteed to strain family relations; usually the lever that produces the buyout rather than the endgame itself. Whatever the route, your sale documents should be executed by you directly (embassy-attested) or under the narrowest possible POA — the inheritance fraud files are full of general POAs given "to complete my sale".

## Money out

Sale proceeds and estate distributions move through normal banking channels; keep the paper aligned — the deed's consideration, the receipts, the transfers — both for Pakistani tax compliance on the transaction and for the foreign bank's source-of-funds questions on arrival. Pakistani property sales by non-residents attract the standard withholding regime; have counsel or a tax adviser compute filer/non-filer positions before the deed, not after.

## The single principle

Every step that requires your signature is a step where you hold power — spend that power deliberately, with your own advice, in writing. The overseas heir who signs slowly and documents quickly finishes with the share the law always intended.
