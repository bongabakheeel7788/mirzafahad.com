Camera enforcement quietly changed the legal relationship between Punjab's drivers and the state: the challan no longer requires a warden's mood, and the record no longer forgets. The system is more contestable than drivers assume and more consequential than they treat it — on both counts, worth twenty minutes of understanding.

## How the system works

The Punjab Safe Cities camera network and its e-challan machinery detect violations — signals, speed, lane and helmet offences among them — read the number plate, and issue a challan against the **registered owner**, delivered by post and SMS and logged against the vehicle in the system. Payment runs through the designated bank and digital channels; verification runs online against the registration or CNIC. Manual challaning by traffic police continues in parallel under the motor-vehicle framework, with its own ticket-and-fine process.

The enforcement teeth are administrative: unpaid e-challans **block the vehicle's services** — token tax payment, transfer of ownership — and accumulate against the owner. The points regime against licences for violations adds the second track: enough violations threaten the licence itself.

## The registered-owner problem

The system's design decision — liability to the *registered* owner — makes Punjab's vast open-letter vehicle market a challan trap on both sides. **Sellers** who never transferred registration receive the buyer's challans indefinitely (and, far worse, the vehicle's criminal history knocks on their door first — a point our property series makes about every unrecorded transfer). **Buyers** on open letters accumulate challans they cannot conveniently contest against a registration that isn't theirs. The cure is the boring one: **record the transfer at excise** promptly on every sale — the fee is trivial against years of someone else's violations. Sellers holding old open-letter sales should regularise them now, not at the first challan.

## Contesting a challan

Wrong challans happen in known patterns: misread plates (the camera's 8 reading as B), **cloned plates** — a real phenomenon with real remedies, engaging the criminal law once demonstrated — challans post-dating a recorded sale, and disputed circumstances. The e-challan carries its **photograph and particulars**: examine them first, because the image answers most disputes one way or the other. The dispute routes run through the system's review channels and the traffic magistracy that stands behind challan enforcement; documented cases — the photo showing a different vehicle, the transfer letter predating the violation — succeed, and a cloned-plate finding should immediately become a criminal complaint, since your plate is now on someone's crime-capable vehicle.

What does not work: ignoring challans on the theory the system forgets. It is precisely the system that no longer forgets.

## Licences, insurance, and the stack

The same counter that pays challans should prompt the annual stack-check: licence current (driving unlicensed converts every incident into a worse one — including for the insurance claims our accident articles discuss), **third-party insurance actually in force** (legally mandatory, practically the difference between a fatal accident's claim landing on a fund or your family), token tax paid, registration matching reality. Every article in this series about accidents, transfers and liability lands eventually on the same principle the e-challan system now enforces monthly: **the record is the reality the law sees — keep yours matching the road.**
