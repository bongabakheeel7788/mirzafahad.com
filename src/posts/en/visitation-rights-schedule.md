When a marriage ends, the fight the children feel most is not about dower or dowry — it is about whether they will see the parent they no longer live with. Pakistani law's answer is the visitation schedule: a court-fixed timetable that converts a vague "you can meet them sometimes" into enforceable dates, hours and places. Parents on both sides handle these cases better once they understand how schedules are made, changed and enforced.

## Custody and visitation are two different orders

Under the Guardians and Wards Act 1890, the guardian court (the family court exercising that jurisdiction) decides custody on one question — the **welfare of the minor**. Customarily the mother has the preferential claim to custody of young children (*hizanat*), while the father remains the natural guardian and bears maintenance regardless of who has custody. Visitation is the counterpart of custody: whichever parent does not have the child is ordinarily entitled to defined contact, because courts consistently hold that a child's welfare includes a relationship with **both** parents. Denying all access to a parent is the exception, reserved for proven danger to the child, not for the bitterness of the parents.

## How a schedule gets fixed

Visitation is usually fixed inside an existing custody or guardianship case — either as an interim arrangement while the case runs, or as part of the final order. The typical lifecycle:

1. **Application** by the non-custodial parent (in a pending case) or a fresh petition where no case exists.
2. **Interim meetings**, often ordered at the first hearings: short, supervised meetings on court premises on hearing dates. These court-corridor meetings are widely criticised as artificial, and judges increasingly move past them quickly.
3. **A structured schedule** in the order: commonly a day or a weekend every fortnight, defined pickup and drop arrangements, a share of summer and winter school holidays, and alternating Eid days. Telephone or video contact is now a normal clause, especially where a parent is overseas.
4. **Tailoring.** Age drives everything: a nursing infant's schedule looks nothing like a twelve-year-old's. School timetables, distance between homes (a Tandlianwala-to-Lahore schedule must be realistic about travel), and the child's health all shape the clauses.

Parents who propose a **specific, workable schedule** in writing — rather than demanding "full visitation" — consistently get better orders.

## Changing a schedule

No visitation order is permanent. Either parent may apply to modify it on **changed circumstances**: the child has started school, a parent has moved cities or abroad, the child's own wishes have matured (courts talk to older children), or the existing schedule has proven unworkable. Overseas Pakistanis commonly seek consolidated blocks — several weeks in summer instead of fortnightly days — and courts grant realistic versions of this, usually with safeguards such as surety bonds or passport conditions against the child being removed from the jurisdiction.

## When visits are denied

Denial is the commonest complaint on both sides: the custodial parent produces excuses, the child is "ill" every meeting date, or handovers collapse into confrontations. The toolkit:

- **Implementation application** before the same guardian court, documenting each missed meeting — dates, messages, witnesses. A written record beats a general grievance.
- **Coercive measures.** The court can warn, order make-up meetings, direct police assistance for handover in stubborn cases, and treat persistent defiance as contemptuous conduct.
- **The custody lever.** Repeated wilful denial of visitation is evidence that the custodial parent is harming the child's welfare — and superior courts have treated it as a ground for reconsidering custody. This is the real deterrent.
- **The mirror rule.** A non-custodial parent who keeps the child beyond the schedule, or disappears with the child, destroys his own case and invites habeas-style proceedings and criminal exposure.

## Conduct that wins these cases

- Keep paying maintenance regardless of visitation disputes — courts notice a father who pays and is refused meetings, and equally one who demands meetings while paying nothing.
- Never use handover moments for arguments; the child is the witness every judge cares about.
- Document everything contemporaneously, and comply strictly with your own obligations under the order before complaining of the other side's breach.

Visitation files are won on realism and records, not on rhetoric. A brief consultation with the existing custody order — or just the facts, if no case exists yet — usually produces a proposed schedule and an enforcement plan in a single sitting.
