Property fraud in Pakistan is not creative. The same seven schemes repeat in district after district, because they keep working on buyers who skip the same checks. Learn the seven and you are protected against most of what the market can do to you.

## 1. The seller who isn't the owner

The classic: an impostor with a genuine-looking CNIC sells another person's land, or a genuine relative sells the whole of a jointly-inherited property. **Defeat it:** obtain the fard yourself from the Arazi Record Centre the same week as the deal; match the CNIC to the recorded owner in person; where the property is inherited, demand the inheritance mutation and confirm every co-heir joins the sale or has partitioned first.

## 2. The double sale

One property, two buyers, two agreements — the fraudster takes advances from both. **Defeat it:** never rest on an agreement alone; move to registration fast; verify at the Sub-Registrar's office whether any prior instrument affects the property; record your possession visibly and immediately. Between competing buyers, priority battles favour the vigilant — the one who registered and took possession.

## 3. The forged or misused power of attorney

Property sold under a POA that is forged, revoked, expired — or genuine but never authorised sale. Overseas owners are the main victims. **Defeat it (as buyer):** insist on the original registered POA, verify it at the registration office where it was registered, confirm the principal is alive (a POA dies with its maker) and, best practice, contact the principal directly; treat gaps ("the original is in Dubai") as deal-breakers. **Defeat it (as owner):** grant narrow, specific, registered POAs and register revocations promptly.

## 4. The oral-gift mutation

A mutation appears in the record claiming the owner orally gifted the land — commonly discovered by heirs after the owner's death, or by owners years after a caretaker relative processed it. **Defeat it:** landowners should periodically pull their own fard (PLRA makes this simple) — annually, and always before and after any family conflict; challenge unknown entries immediately, because time builds the fraud's paper trail.

## 5. The housing society file game

Files for plots that don't exist, in phases never approved, sold and resold; societies without approved layout plans (LDA/TMA/authority approvals) collecting instalments for years. **Defeat it:** verify the society's approval status with the development authority directly; demand the plot's position on the *approved* map, not the brochure; check the society's own record of the file's ownership chain and any dues; prefer possession-ready plots; and treat "guaranteed ballot" and heavy-discount pre-launch offers as the red flags they are.

## 6. Sale of encumbered or litigated property

The land is mortgaged to a bank, attached by a court, or mid-litigation — disclosed nowhere. **Defeat it:** search at the Sub-Registrar for registered mortgages; check the fard's encumbrance columns; ask the local civil court records (a lawyer's day of work) where suspicion exists; and put a warranty in the sale deed that the property is free of encumbrance and litigation — with the seller's liability spelled out.

## 7. The stay-order squeeze

You buy, you start construction, and a relative of the seller appears with a suit and a stay order — a manufactured dispute designed to be bought off. **Defeat it:** the pre-purchase checks above shrink this risk; if it lands anyway, contest the injunction on its merits quickly (manufactured suits are weak at the interim stage) rather than paying quietly, which marks you for the next round.

## The pattern behind all seven

Every scheme exploits a gap between paper and reality: record vs occupant, agreement vs registration, brochure vs approved plan. The universal defence is verification at the source — the record centre, the registrar, the authority, the property itself — before money moves. The few thousand rupees and days this costs is the cheapest insurance in the entire economy. And if you have already been caught: act at once — remedies (cancellation suits, criminal complaints, injunctions of your own) reward speed and wither with delay.
