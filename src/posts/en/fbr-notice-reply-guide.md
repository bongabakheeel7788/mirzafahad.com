Most FBR notices today arrive silently — inside your IRIS account, with a copy to the email and mobile number registered against your NTN. People discover them months later, after the deadline has passed and an order has already been made. The first habit of a safe taxpayer is simply checking IRIS; the second is treating every notice as a document that must be answered in writing, within time, on the record.

## The notices you are most likely to see

**Section 114(4) — file your return.** The Commissioner believes you were required to file a return for a tax year and did not. The notice gives a date by which to file. This is the most common notice for non-filers whose bank transactions, property purchases or vehicle registrations have surfaced in FBR's data.

**Section 122 — amendment of assessment.** Your filed return is treated as an assessment, but the Commissioner can amend it where definite information suggests income was concealed or the declared version is wrong. Before amending, a show-cause notice under section 122(9) of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 must issue, telling you what is alleged and inviting your explanation. This is the notice that deserves the most careful, document-backed reply, because the order that follows creates an actual demand.

**Section 176 — produce information or documents.** A fact-finding notice: bank statements, contracts, ledgers, details of a specific transaction. It often precedes a 122 notice, so the quality of your answer here shapes everything after.

You may also meet **section 177** (selection for audit) and **section 182** (show cause for penalty). The pattern is the same throughout: allegation or query, deadline, opportunity to explain, then an order.

## Read the notice before reacting

Four things to extract, ideally with your adviser on the same screen:

1. **The section and tax year.** The section tells you the legal track; the year tells you which records to pull.
2. **The exact allegation or requirement.** A 122(9) notice must state the definite information relied on — a property transaction, a bank credit, a withholding mismatch. Your reply answers that, not generalities.
3. **The compliance date.** Deadlines in the notice are real. They can be extended on request, but only before they lapse.
4. **The officer and jurisdiction.** Replies go to the issuing officer through IRIS; a wrong jurisdiction can itself be a ground to raise.

## How a proper reply is built

Reply through IRIS against the notice itself, so the response is part of the electronic record. A sound reply states the facts plainly, attaches the documents that prove them — bank statements, registered deeds, withholding certificates, remittance proceeds through banking channels for overseas money — and addresses each allegation specifically rather than with blanket denial. Where a transaction is explainable (a gift from a parent, sale proceeds of declared property, a loan received through a crossed cheque), say so and attach the trail. Where you genuinely owe something, a corrected return with payment is often cheaper than contesting a hopeless point, because default surcharge keeps running.

If the date is too tight, seek an adjournment through IRIS before expiry. Officers grant reasonable requests; appellate forums later look kindly on taxpayers who engaged and badly on those who vanished.

## The cost of silence

Ignore a 114(4) notice and the Commissioner may complete a **best-judgment assessment under section 121** — your income estimated from the department's data, invariably on the high side. Ignore a 122(9) show cause and the amendment is made ex parte. Penalties under section 182 and default surcharge under section 205 attach to the demand, and recovery measures — including notices to your bank under section 140 — can follow a demand that has become final. Unwinding an ex parte order through appeal to the Commissioner (Appeals) and the Appellate Tribunal is possible but slower and costlier than answering the original notice ever was.

## Keep the long game in view

Every reply you file becomes part of your tax history, and consistency between your returns, wealth statement and replies is what keeps future notices away. In practice, most notices around Faisalabad arise from property and banking data mismatches that a well-documented reply resolves at the first stage.

A short consultation with the notice, your relevant returns and the underlying documents — bank statements, deeds, certificates — settles the strategy in almost every case: reply and contest, revise and pay, or seek time and prepare.
