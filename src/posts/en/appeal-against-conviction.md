A conviction feels like the end; in law it is the end of one round. Pakistan's criminal system builds in at least one full appeal on facts and law, and for the days or years the appeal takes, a separate mechanism — suspension of sentence — decides whether the convict waits in jail or at home. What matters most in the first week after conviction is speed and sequence.

## Where the appeal lies

The forum follows the court that convicted:

- **Conviction by a Magistrate** → appeal to the **Court of Session** under **section 408 CrPC** (with limited exceptions routed elsewhere).
- **Conviction by the Court of Session** → appeal to the **High Court** under **section 410 CrPC**.
- **Death sentence** → even without an appeal, the sentence cannot be executed until the **High Court confirms** it under section 374; in practice the murder reference and the convict's appeal are heard together.
- Onward, a further challenge lies to the **Supreme Court**, by appeal or leave under Article 185 of the Constitution — a narrower, law-focused stage.

Acquittals can also be appealed — by the state or, with leave, the complainant under **section 417 CrPC** — which is why an acquitted accused is not entirely out of the woods until that window closes.

## The clock

Criminal limitation is unforgiving and short: as a working rule, **30 days** for appeals to the Sessions Court and **60 days** to the High Court, with shorter special periods in certain cases, the time spent obtaining certified copies excluded. Two practical commands follow. **Apply for certified copies of the judgment the same day** — the copy application's date anchors your limitation computation. And **do not wait for the mercy-or-appeal family debate to settle**: a convict in jail can file through jail authorities, and counsel can file with the attested judgment copy at once. Condonation of delay exists but is discretionary; nobody should plan around it.

## Suspension of sentence and bail pending appeal

Filing the appeal does not itself open the jail door. The appellate court may, under **section 426 CrPC**, **suspend the sentence** and admit the appellant to **bail pending appeal**. Courts weigh, among other things:

- **Length of sentence versus length of the queue.** In short sentences — a year or two — where the appeal cannot realistically be heard before the sentence is served, suspension is the norm rather than the exception; otherwise the appeal's success would be meaningless.
- **Apparent merit.** A conviction resting on visibly shaky evidence — a single interested witness, a hostile medical report — invites suspension even in longer sentences.
- **Conduct.** An appellant who was on bail during trial and never misused it stands on far better footing.
- **Nature of the offence.** In heinous offences and long sentences, courts are slower, and statutory restrictions and prosecutorial opposition weigh in.

Fines and compensation orders can also be suspended, and the court can stay ancillary consequences while the appeal pends.

## What the appellate court can do

A first criminal appeal is a **re-hearing on the record**: the court re-reads the entire evidence and can reach its own conclusions. Its powers are wide — **acquit**, **maintain the conviction**, **alter the conviction** to a lesser offence, **reduce the sentence**, or order a **retrial** where the trial was vitiated. Enhancement of sentence is possible only through a proper notice or a separate revision/appeal by the other side, with the convict heard. Many convictions are not demolished but downgraded on appeal — murder to qatl under a lesser head, with sentence adjusted accordingly — so the realistic goals of an appeal should be set honestly at the outset.

## Building the appeal

Strong criminal appeals are built from the trial record, not rhetoric: contradictions between ocular and medical evidence, improvements over 161 statements, unexplained delay in the FIR, defective identification, recoveries that violate safeguards, circumstances never put to the accused under section 342. The appeal's grounds should be drafted from a full reading of the paper book — which is why the certified copies ordered on day one matter twice.

If a conviction has just landed on your family, the week ahead decides the next year: forum, limitation, suspension application and grounds all need to move together. A brief consultation with the judgment, the order on sentence and the trial record settles the sequence — and whether bail pending appeal is a realistic first target.
