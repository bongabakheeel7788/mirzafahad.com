The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 (PECA) is the law most ordinary Pakistanis now encounter before any other — through a hacked account, a blackmail message, a fake profile, or money that vanished through a "bank helpline" call. The complaint process works better than its reputation, but it rewards those who arrive with evidence preserved and the right offence identified.

## What PECA covers

The frequently-invoked provisions criminalise: **unauthorised access** to systems and data (hacking, account takeovers); **electronic fraud and forgery** — the legal home of bank-fraud calls, OTP scams and fake online sellers; offences against **dignity and privacy**: transmitting false information to harm reputation, and the gravely-charged offences around sharing intimate images or fabricated images — the blackmail provisions that protect, above all, women targeted with real or edited photographs; **cyberstalking and harassment** — persistent unwanted contact, monitoring, threats; and identity-related offences covering **fake profiles** and unauthorised use of another's identity. Penalties scale sharply where victims are women or minors, and several offences carry enhanced treatment in aggravated forms.

## Before anything: preserve, don't delete

The instinct of the harassed — block and delete — destroys the case. Preserve first: **screenshots including the profile URL, handle, date and time**; screen recordings of the content in situ; the offending numbers, accounts, transaction IDs and bank details; and your own device untouched. Then block. Complaints stand on this material, and platforms' data may be obtainable later only through formal channels whose success varies — your captured record is the reliable core.

## Filing the complaint

Cybercrime complaints go to the specialised federal machinery — the cybercrime wing long housed in the **FIA** and, under the recent restructuring, the **National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA)**: online through the complaint portal, or in person at the regional cybercrime circles (Lahore and Faisalabad serve this region). A complaint states the facts, attaches the preserved evidence, and identifies the suspect where known. On verification, an **enquiry** is opened; on the enquiry confirming a cognizable offence, an **FIR** is registered under the relevant PECA sections, with arrests, device forensics and prosecutions before the designated courts following.

Set expectations honestly: volumes are enormous, enquiry stages can be slow, and follow-up matters — written reminders, appearance with counsel, and escalation to circle in-charges move files. For urgent ongoing harm (an active blackmail campaign), press the urgency in person and in writing; interim outcomes like content takedown and platform reporting run in parallel through the PTA's reporting channels and the platforms' own mechanisms.

## Financial fraud: the clock is everything

For frauds through banking channels, the first hour matters more than the first week: call your bank's helpline immediately, dispute the transaction, and demand freezing of the beneficiary account — recovery, when it happens, happens because the chain froze before withdrawal. File the cybercrime complaint the same day with the transaction trail, and complain in parallel to the **State Bank's Sunwai portal / banking ombudsman** where the bank's own negligence (SIM-swap, unauthorised transfers) is in play. Never share OTPs, and treat every "your account will be blocked" call as the fraud it is.

## If you are accused

PECA's breadth cuts both ways: business disputes and personal vendettas arrive as cybercrime complaints too. Respond through counsel from the enquiry stage — the enquiry is where wrongly-directed complaints are best terminated; do not volunteer devices or passwords without legal advice; and note that bail jurisprudence under PECA's mostly sub-ten-year offences favours release pending trial.

Online harm feels lawless; it isn't. The pipeline — preserve, complain, follow up, escalate — delivers real outcomes, most reliably for the complainant whose evidence file was built in the first day and whose follow-through never let the file sleep.
