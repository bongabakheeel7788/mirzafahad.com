Khula is the right of a Muslim wife to seek dissolution of her marriage through the court when she can no longer live with her husband "within the limits prescribed by Allah". Unlike talaq, which the husband pronounces, khula is granted by a judge. In Punjab it is one of the most common family matters, and the procedure is more straightforward than many women fear.

## Who can apply

Any wife may file a suit for khula in the Family Court. She does not need her husband's consent, and she does not need to prove fault such as cruelty or desertion. The test the court applies is whether the spouses can live together as the law requires. Her own firm statement that she cannot, is ordinarily sufficient.

## Where to file

Under the **Family Courts Act, 1964**, the suit is filed in the Family Court of the district where the wife *ordinarily resides*. A woman living with her parents in Faisalabad after leaving her husband's home in another city may file in Faisalabad. This matters, because it means she does not have to travel to her husband's district for every hearing.

## Documents you will need

- Copy of the *nikahnama* (marriage certificate). If you do not have it, a copy can be obtained from the Union Council where the nikah was registered.
- Copy of your CNIC.
- Copies of children's B-forms, if you also claim custody or maintenance.
- Any evidence relevant to dower (*haq mehr*) — the nikahnama's columns 13–16 record what was fixed and what was paid.

## The procedure, step by step

1. **Plaint filed.** A plaint (written claim) is prepared stating the marriage details, the reasons the wife cannot continue in the marriage, and what she asks for — dissolution by khula, and often maintenance, recovery of dowry articles or custody in the same suit.
2. **Summons to the husband.** The court issues notice. If he does not appear after proper service, the court may proceed *ex parte*.
3. **Written statement.** The husband files his reply. He may contest, or he may not.
4. **Pre-trial reconciliation.** The judge is required to attempt reconciliation. This is a genuine stage — the court will ask both parties whether they can resolve matters — but it cannot be used to force a wife to stay.
5. **Decree.** If reconciliation fails, the court passes a decree of dissolution by khula. In practice, in a suit for khula alone, this often happens at or shortly after the pre-trial stage, without a lengthy trial.
6. **Union Council.** The decree is sent to the Union Council (Arbitration Council), which issues a certificate of dissolution after the *iddat* period of 90 days. It is this certificate that formally records the end of the marriage for NADRA and remarriage purposes.

## What happens to dower

This is the question wives ask most. Under section 10(4) of the Family Courts Act, when khula is decreed the wife must return the dower (*haq mehr*) that she has *received*, up to a maximum of 50%. If the dower was fixed but never paid, there is generally nothing to return. Prompt dower that was actually paid may have to be returned in part. Gifts and dowry articles given by her own family are not dower and are not returned — in fact, she can claim them back.

The details vary with the facts of the marriage and the wording of the nikahnama, which is why a lawyer should read the nikahnama before the plaint is filed.

## Timeline

A contested khula in a district court in Punjab typically takes **three to six months**. Where the husband does not appear or does not seriously contest, it can be faster. Adding claims for maintenance, dowry articles or custody in the same suit is efficient but usually extends the case, because those issues require evidence.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Filing in the wrong court.** Filing where the husband lives, rather than where the wife resides, adds travel and delay for no benefit.
- **Leaving out related claims.** Maintenance for children, past maintenance for the wife, and dowry articles can be claimed in the same suit. Filing separately later means a second case from scratch.
- **Not collecting the Union Council certificate.** The decree alone is not the end. Until the certificate issues, NADRA records still show the marriage.

## After the decree

Once the certificate is issued, the wife is free to remarry after *iddat*. Custody and maintenance orders made in the same suit continue to apply and can be enforced through the same court if the husband defaults.

If you are considering khula, the most useful first step is a short consultation with your nikahnama in hand. Most of what determines the case — the court, the dower position, the related claims — can be settled in that first conversation.
