A termination letter lands with no warning, the final salary is short, and the HR officer says "company policy" as if that ends the matter. It does not. For a large class of private employees in Punjab, the law prescribes exactly how a job may be ended — and an employer who skips the procedure can be ordered to take you back with back pay. The catch is that the deadlines for complaining are brutally short.

## First question: are you a "workman"?

Pakistani labour law protects the **workman** — broadly, employees doing manual, skilled, technical or clerical work in commercial and industrial establishments. The **Industrial and Commercial Employment (Standing Orders) Ordinance 1968** applies to establishments above a modest employee threshold and writes the ground rules of the relationship into law, whatever the appointment letter says.

Purely managerial employees and those in very small establishments generally fall outside this regime; their remedy is a civil suit on the contract, usually for damages rather than reinstatement. Government servants have their own service tribunals. Everything below concerns the private-sector workman, which covers most factory, shop, mill and office staff in Faisalabad's industrial economy.

## Termination simpliciter: notice or pay

An employer may end a permanent workman's employment without alleging any fault — but the Standing Orders require **one month's written notice or one month's wages in lieu**, and the reason must be stated in writing. On termination, all earned dues follow: outstanding wages, leave encashment, and gratuity or provident fund where applicable.

Two common illegalities to spot:

- **"Terminated" as punishment in disguise.** If the letter says termination simpliciter but the real reason is alleged misconduct, courts look through the label — an employer cannot dodge the inquiry requirement by paying a month's wages.
- **Keeping workers "temporary" forever.** A workman kept on fictitious temporary or contract status for years, doing permanent work, is treated by the courts as permanent in substance.

## Dismissal for misconduct: the due process

Misconduct — theft, wilful disobedience, habitual absence, negligence causing loss — permits dismissal **without** notice pay, but only after a procedure the Standing Orders fix:

1. A **written charge sheet** stating the specific allegations.
2. A real opportunity to submit a **written explanation**.
3. A **domestic inquiry** by an unbiased officer, where the workman can hear the evidence and defend himself.
4. A **show-cause** and a reasoned dismissal order.

Skip any step and the dismissal is vulnerable regardless of whether the misconduct actually happened. Labour Courts set aside procedurally bad dismissals every week; the inquiry file, not the allegation, decides most cases.

## The grievance route and the Labour Court

Under the **Punjab Industrial Relations Act 2010**, a workman aggrieved by termination serves a **grievance notice** on the employer, in writing, within the short statutory period after the termination. If the employer does not redress the grievance within the time allowed, the workman petitions the **Labour Court**.

Mark this well: the limitation periods at both steps are counted in **days, not months**. More good cases die of delay than of weak merits. The practical sequence for a terminated worker in Faisalabad is simple — collect the termination letter, appointment letter, salary slips or bank statements, and attendance proof; serve the grievance notice immediately; and file in the Labour Court the moment the redressal window closes.

## Reinstatement or compensation

Where termination or dismissal is held wrongful, the Labour Court can order **reinstatement with back benefits** — the classic remedy, treating the service as unbroken. Where reinstatement is impractical (the unit closed, the relationship poisoned, long time elapsed), the court awards **compensation** instead. Employers' appeals lie to the Labour Appellate Tribunal and onward, so finality takes time, but a reinstatement order carries real force, and back-benefit liability grows while the employer litigates.

## What to do in the first week

- Do **not** sign a resignation, settlement or "full and final" receipt under pressure; a signed voluntary resignation forecloses almost everything.
- Take copies of everything: appointment letter, letters, warnings, the charge sheet if any.
- Note witnesses to how the termination happened.
- Serve the grievance notice through counsel within days.

Termination cases are won on documents and deadlines. A brief consultation with your appointment letter, termination letter and salary record in hand will establish within minutes whether you have a reinstatement case, a dues-recovery case, or both — and starts the clock-critical notice on time.
