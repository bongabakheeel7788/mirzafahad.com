The price you negotiate for a plot is not the price you pay. Between the bargain and the registered deed stands a stack of government levies — stamp duty, registration fee, local transfer fee, federal withholding tax — and two different official values your deed will be measured against. Buyers who treat this stack as a nuisance to minimise often buy themselves a problem that surfaces years later, in court, at the worst possible moment.

## The components of the stack

On a typical Punjab sale deed, four kinds of charges arise:

- **Stamp duty** under the **Stamp Act 1899** (as amended for Punjab) — the main provincial levy on the instrument itself, charged as a percentage of the property's value. Urban and rural rates have differed, and governments adjust the rate in nearly every budget cycle.
- **Registration fee** under the Registration Act 1908 — paid to register the deed with the sub-registrar.
- **Local transfer fee** — a percentage levied by the local government (the old "TMA fee") on transfers within its limits.
- **Federal withholding income tax** on the purchase and sale under the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 — collected at registration, with rates that differ sharply for filers and non-filers.

None of these figures is worth memorising, because all of them move. What does not change is the structure: before executing a deed, have the current schedule computed for your specific property — urban or rural, its recorded value, your filer status — rather than relying on last year's percentages or the property dealer's estimate.

## Which value the duty is charged on

Duty is not charged on whatever price you write. Punjab maintains **district collector (DC) valuation tables**, and the FBR notifies its own values for withholding tax purposes. Your deed is assessed on the declared price or the applicable table value, **whichever is higher**. Writing a token price below the table achieves nothing except an inaccurate document; writing a price far below what you actually paid creates a different problem — your own deed understates the consideration you may one day need to prove, and unexplained cash sits outside your tax record.

## e-Stamping: how payment now works

Punjab moved high-value stamps to an electronic system. The process in practice:

1. Generate the e-Stamp challan online through the Punjab e-Stamping portal, entering the property details, parties and value — the system computes the duty from the applicable tables.
2. Pay the challan at the designated bank.
3. Receive the printed **e-Stamp paper** carrying a unique ID that the sub-registrar (and anyone else) can verify online.

This killed the classic frauds of the vendor era — counterfeit stamp papers and back-dated papers "arranged" for fabricated documents. It also means the duty calculation is largely out of the parties' hands, which protects honest buyers.

## What under-stamping actually costs

The Stamp Act has teeth, and they bite late:

- **Impounding:** an authority or court that receives an instrument not duly stamped must impound it (section 33).
- **Inadmissibility:** under section 35, an under-stamped instrument **cannot be received in evidence** until the deficient duty is paid together with a penalty — which can run to a multiple of the deficiency.
- **Practical translation:** the day your seller's brother files a suit disputing the sale is the day your bargain-priced deed becomes a document you must cure, at a penalty, before the court will even look at it.

The few thousand rupees saved by understating value or skimping on duty are borrowed, not saved, at a brutal interest rate.

## A buyer's checklist before the sub-registrar

- Confirm the current rates — stamp, registration, local fee, withholding tax — for this property this month.
- Confirm the applicable DC/FBR table values; budget on the higher of table and price.
- Check your and the seller's **filer status**; the withholding difference is substantial and is one of the quiet arguments for filing returns.
- Insist on genuine e-Stamp papers and keep the challan and verification printout in the file.
- Declare the real consideration. The deed is your title document for the next fifty years; do not weaken it to shave a fee today.

Every property's stack computes differently — urban versus rural, constructed versus plot, gift versus sale, exemptions that occasionally apply within families. A brief consultation with the fard and the proposed deed in hand will settle the exact figures and the registration strategy before you commit.
