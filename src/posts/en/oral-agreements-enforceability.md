"We agreed in front of the whole baithak — everyone knows the deal happened." Half the civil litigation in tehsil courts begins with a sentence like this, and much of it ends with a judgment explaining that knowing a deal happened and proving its terms are different things. The law's answer to verbal agreements is split: most are valid, many are unprovable, and for land in Punjab the door has been closed almost completely.

## The general rule: oral contracts bind

The Contract Act 1872 defines a contract by substance, not form: a lawful offer, acceptance, consideration and parties competent to contract. Nothing in the general law requires writing. A verbal agreement to supply cloth, repair a machine, lend money, or share a harvest is a real contract, and a court can enforce it or award damages for its breach — **if** its existence and terms are established in evidence.

So the common belief "zabani baat ki koi haisiyat nahin" is legally wrong but practically half-right. The status exists; the proof usually does not.

## The great exception: immovable property

For land, houses and plots, the law has moved decisively against oral dealing:

- The Transfer of Property Act 1882 requires a sale of immovable property (above a token value, which covers essentially all real transactions) to be made by a **registered instrument**.
- In Punjab, the **2015 amendments to the land revenue framework** ended the era of oral sales recorded straight into mutations: a mutation of sale now rides on a **registered deed**, with the parties' biometric verification in the process. The old practice — two men appear before the revenue officer, declare a bargain, and the patwari's record changes hands — is gone.
- Family transactions still produce "oral gift" claims, because Muslim law recognises oral hiba (declaration, acceptance, delivery of possession). These remain litigable in principle, but courts examine belated oral-gift claims — typically surfacing after the donor's death, typically cutting out daughters — with deep suspicion, and the beneficiary must prove each ingredient strictly.

The practical message for buyers: paying on a handshake, or on a bare receipt, for immovable property acquires you a lawsuit, not a title. The agreement-to-sell stage can be documented and enforceable; the transfer itself must be a registered deed.

## Proving an oral agreement when you must

Where you are already in an oral deal gone wrong, the case is built from three materials:

1. **Witnesses.** People present at the bargain, testifying to its specific terms — not merely that "a deal existed". Their consistency under cross-examination is usually where these cases are won or lost.
2. **Conduct.** Performance consistent only with the alleged agreement: possession delivered, instalments paid, goods supplied, work done. Courts reason backwards from conduct to contract.
3. **Edge documents.** The deal was oral but its shadow rarely is: bank transfers, mobile wallet entries, receipts, ledgers (business books kept in the ordinary course have evidentiary value), WhatsApp messages and call records referring to the bargain. Under the Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984, where a transaction *was* reduced to writing, its terms are proved by the document itself and oral evidence cannot vary them — which cuts the other way too: your opponent's partial documents can trap an oral narrative.

The burden sits on whoever asserts the agreement, and courts weigh oral claims cautiously precisely because they are easy to invent. Expect strict scrutiny of dates, amounts and the witnesses' relationship to you.

## Why writing wins even among family

The standard objection — "likhat parhat rishtedari mein buri lagti hai" — mistakes what a written document does. It does not signal distrust; it prevents the dispute that destroys the relationship later, when memories diverge honestly and heirs inherit positions instead of understandings. A one-page agreement with CNIC numbers, amounts, dates and two witnesses costs an hour. For money matters, a cheque or bank transfer adds a self-dating proof. For anything touching land, nothing below a registered document deserves your money.

If you are owed under a verbal deal, move early: limitation periods run regardless of how the contract was made, and evidence — witnesses' availability, messages, records — decays fast. A brief consultation with whatever fragments exist (payments, chats, witness names) usually shows whether the claim is provable and which forum fits it.
