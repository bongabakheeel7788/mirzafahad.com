Pre-emption — shufa — gives certain persons the right to step into a completed sale of immovable property and take it for themselves at the sale price. It is an ancient right with a modern statute, and it is the most deadline-driven area of property law in Punjab: strong claims die on missed timelines more often than on merits.

## Who has the right

Under the Punjab Pre-emption Act, 1991, the right belongs, in order of priority, to categories including the **co-sharer in the property** (shafi-i-sharik), the participator in special immunities and appendages such as a shared passage or water course (**shafi-i-khalit**), and the **owner of adjoining property** (shafi-i-jar). A superior category excludes an inferior one; among equals, the court balances statutory rules. The right attaches to **sales** — not gifts, inheritance, exchanges within limits, or certain transfers the Act exempts. And a pre-emptor must hold the qualification **at the time of the sale** and maintain the claim's requirements throughout.

## The talbs: the heart of every case

The Act codifies the classical demands (talbs), and non-compliance with any is fatal:

1. **Talb-i-muwathibat** — the immediate demand. The moment the pre-emptor learns of the sale, he must declare his intention to pre-empt. Case law treats this as required *promptly on knowledge* — delay measured in days, sometimes less, defeats it. Its proof (when, where, before whom the words were spoken) is the most litigated fact in shufa trials.
2. **Talb-i-ishhad** — the confirmatory demand: within **two weeks** of the first demand, notice affirming the intention, made with witnesses, and under the Act communicated to the vendee — the settled practice being a **written notice, attested by two truthful witnesses, dispatched by registered post acknowledgment due**. Defects here — unwitnessed notices, wrong addressees, missing postal proof — end suits.
3. **Talb-i-khusumat** — the demand by suit: filing the pre-emption suit within the limitation the law provides.

Courts apply these requirements strictly: pre-emption is a *right to displace a lawful buyer*, so the pre-emptor must be punctilious. Plead each talb with its date, time, place and witnesses; prove them exactly as pleaded.

## Price and deposit

The pre-emptor takes at the **actual sale price** — and the statutory scheme requires the pre-emptor to deposit a substantial portion (the Act fixes the fraction) of the probable price in court when filing. Disputes over understated registered prices cut both ways: buyers who understated to save stamp duty find pre-emptors offering the paper price; courts determine the true consideration on evidence.

## The buyer's defences

A vendee resisting pre-emption works down a checklist: attack the pre-emptor's **qualification** (is he truly a co-sharer/adjoining owner of the *sold* parcel?); attack **each talb** — timing of knowledge, the immediate demand's reality, the notice's formalities, limitation; examine **estoppel and waiver** — a pre-emptor who negotiated to buy, congratulated the sale, or stood by during construction; and structural defences arising from the transaction's form within the Act's exemptions. Buyers anticipating shufa exposure sometimes see sellers structure transactions accordingly — an area where advice before the deal is worth far more than defence after it.

## Practical notes

For potential pre-emptors: treat knowledge of the sale as a fire alarm — make the immediate demand before witnesses the same day, complete the written witnessed notice within the fortnight, and file within limitation with the deposit ready. For buyers: complete quickly and visibly; open possession and construction start the clocks of knowledge running and build equities. For both: shufa cases are won by whoever respected the calendar.
