Mobile wallets banked millions of Pakistanis whom banks never reached — and created the country's busiest fraud arena, where the same six scripts harvest daily. The defence and recovery playbook is short; the scripts are shorter.

## The scripts

**The prize call.** "Aap ka inaam nikla hai" — BISP, a game show, a telco anniversary — claim it by sharing the code just SMS'd, which is the wallet's OTP; the account empties in the minute after. Every OTP request from a caller, without exception, is theft in progress.

**The fake credit / wrong transfer.** A counterfeit "received Rs 9,500" SMS, then the tearful call: wrong number, please return it. The app's actual history shows no credit — the "return" would be the theft. Balances live in apps, never in SMS.

**The SIM swap.** Fraudsters obtain a replacement SIM for your number; the wallet's SMS security now flows to them. Sudden signal death that isn't a network outage is an emergency: call the telco from another line at once. Telco-side negligence in issuing the swap is a recognised complaint ground.

**Agent-side fraud.** Deposits pocketed with fake confirmations, "system error" double-charges, withdrawal shortchanging. Transact against the app's own confirmation on your screen, never the agent's word or printed slip alone.

**The account-rental trap** — worth its own warning: "commission" offers to receive funds through your wallet recruit you as the fraud chain's launderer; the account that receives stolen money is the one the investigation reaches first, and "I only kept ten percent" is a confession.

## Recovery: the golden hour

The moment fraud lands: call the provider's **helpline** (JazzCash and Easypaisa both run 24/7 lines) — report, demand the **transaction's beneficiary be flagged and frozen**, and get the complaint number. Wallet-to-wallet theft sits recoverable for exactly as long as the money hasn't been cashed out at an agent; providers can and do freeze in time when reports beat withdrawal. Same day: the **cybercrime complaint** (portal or regional circle — Faisalabad's serves this region) with transaction IDs, the fraud number, and your evidence, as our cybercrime article details; and where a bank account sat in the chain, that bank's fraud line too.

Unresolved or mishandled by the provider — refused freezes, agent fraud denied, SIM-swap negligence — escalate on the **regulatory track**: these operators run under State Bank branchless-banking supervision, and its consumer-protection channels and the Mohtasib framework receive what the helplines bury. The telco's own complaint machinery, and PTA above it, take the SIM-swap dimension.

## The settings that end most risk

App PIN different from phone PIN, and told to no one — family included, since "family fraud" fills a sad share of these files. Biometric login on. Transaction alerts on. The account's registered SIM physically in your possession, its telco account secured against remote replacement. And the reflex that defeats every script at once: **no caller — provider, bank, army officer, prize committee — ever legitimately needs an OTP, PIN, or "verification transfer".** The scripts survive because each victim believes their call was different. It never is.
