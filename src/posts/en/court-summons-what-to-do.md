A man appears at your door with a printed form, a case number and your name — or the summons arrives by post, or is pasted on your gate. The next hour usually goes to panic, and the hour after to bad advice from relatives. Here is the calm version: what the paper means, what it does not mean, and the sequence that protects you.

## What a summons is — and is not

A summons is the court's formal direction that you appear, as defendant or accused or witness, in a specific case on a specific date. In civil matters it issues under **Order V of the CPC 1908**, usually with a copy of the plaint attached; in criminal matters under the **CrPC 1898**, naming the offence complained of. A summons is **not** a finding against you, not an arrest warrant, and not a verdict that a case is strong. Courts summon on the plaintiff's or complainant's version alone — your side enters the record only after you appear.

Read the document itself before reacting: the **court** and its location, the **case number and title** (who is suing or accusing you), the **capacity** in which you are called (party or mere witness — radically different situations), the **next date**, and what you must do (appear, file a written statement, produce a document).

## Verify it first

Summonses are forged for extortion and intimidation. Verification is quick:

- Punjab's district judiciary runs **online case-status systems** — search by case number or party name to confirm the case exists and its next date.
- The concerned court's **reader or ahlmad** can confirm a listing from the register.
- Treat with total suspicion any "process server" or caller who offers to settle or bury the case for cash. Genuine court staff do not negotiate cases at your doorstep.

## What ignoring it costs

The law is built so that silence hurts only you:

- **Civil:** on proven service and your non-appearance, the court proceeds **ex parte**. The plaintiff leads evidence unopposed and can obtain an ex parte decree — against which your remedies (setting aside under Order IX Rule 13, appeal) are time-barred, discretionary and uphill. People have lost houses defending nothing.
- **Criminal:** non-appearance converts a summons case into a warrant problem — bailable warrants, then non-bailable warrants, then **proclamation as an absconder** with attachment of property under the CrPC. A defensible case becomes a fugitive file.
- **Witness:** even a witness who ignores summonses faces coercive process; attendance is a legal duty, not a favour.

Never assume defective service protects you. "I never received it" is an argument for later litigation, not a plan.

## The right sequence

1. **Photograph and keep everything** — summons, envelope, attached copies — and note how and when it was served.
2. **See a lawyer before the date**, with the summons and every document connected to the underlying dispute. The first hearing's posture — appearance, objections, whether to seek time — is strategy, not formality.
3. **Sign a wakalatnama** so counsel appears for you. In civil cases that usually spares you routine attendance; in criminal matters discuss bail (where the offence requires it) *before* the date, so you walk in protected rather than react in the courtroom.
4. **Meet the deadlines the summons starts.** A civil summons typically triggers the window for your written statement; missing it can forfeit your right to defend on your own terms.
5. **Track the case yourself** on the online system alongside your counsel — dates move, and a missed adjourned date does the same damage as a missed first one.

## If you are called as a witness

Attend, take the relevant documents the summons specifies, answer truthfully, and take counsel's advice beforehand if your testimony could touch your own interests. Witness summonses in family and civil cases are routine machinery, not accusations.

## The underlying case still has to be fought

Appearing is step zero; the dispute behind the summons — the loan, the plot, the family fight, the FIR — needs its own strategy of defence, counterclaim or settlement. What the summons gives you is the timetable. A brief consultation with the summons and the underlying documents settles most of that strategy in one sitting: whether to contest, compromise or counter-attack, and what must be filed before the date printed on that single page.
