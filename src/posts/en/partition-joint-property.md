Inheritance makes co-owners; partition makes peace. Between the two lies the most protracted category of family litigation in Punjab. Understanding the machinery of partition — which forum, what outcomes are possible, what each co-sharer can and cannot do meanwhile — shortens the road considerably.

## Co-ownership: what it means day to day

Until partition, every co-sharer owns an undivided fraction of **every inch** of the property, not any particular corner — even if family practice has one brother in the house and another on the land. Consequences: a co-sharer in exclusive possession is ordinarily treated as possessing on behalf of all (his possession does not become hostile without unequivocal ouster); any co-sharer may **sell his undivided share** without the others' consent (the buyer steps into co-ownership — and may face pre-emption); and no co-sharer may unilaterally change the property's character — new construction over joint land invites injunctions and, at partition, equitable adjustment.

## Forum one: revenue partition for agricultural land

For agricultural land, partition proceedings lie before the **revenue officer** under the Land Revenue Act. Any co-sharer applies; notice goes to all; objections to *title* can push parties to civil court, but where shares are undisputed (the usual case after an inheritance mutation), the revenue officer frames a **partition scheme (naqsha)** through field staff — balancing quality of land, water sources, access and existing possession — and executes it, creating separate khatas. It is cheaper and, when pursued diligently, faster than civil litigation. Its weakness is susceptibility to adjournment and influence at the tehsil level; the cure is persistent representation and, where needed, revisions to senior revenue authorities.

## Forum two: the civil partition suit

Houses, urban plots and mixed estates go to the **civil court** in a suit for partition and separate possession. The court first decides shares (a formality where inheritance is settled), then passes a **preliminary decree** declaring them, and then the mechanics: division by metes and bounds through a local commission where the property can be split; where it cannot — one house, many heirs — the established mechanism is valuation and sale, with provisions allowing a co-sharer to buy out others at valuation, or **auction** among the co-sharers or the public, distributing proceeds by shares. Courts prefer arrangements letting family members retain property against payment over forcing sales to strangers, but a co-sharer insisting on the property itself where division is impossible cannot block liquidation forever.

## The recurring battlefronts

**"We already divided it privately."** An acted-upon oral family settlement — separate possession for years, separate constructions, separate utility connections — carries real weight and can be recorded formally. But half-settlements (divided possession, undivided record) are unstable: record follows possession only through partition or registered instruments, and the heirs of tomorrow reopen what the brothers of today left undocumented.

**Mesne profits.** A co-sharer kept out of a income-yielding property (rented shops, productive land) can claim his share of the profits for the period of exclusion — a claim worth pleading from the start, since it changes settlement mathematics.

**Improvements.** The brother who built a floor on the joint house gets equitable credit at partition — but building to engineer a larger share is transparent to courts.

## Practical course

Start with the record: confirmed shares (inheritance mutation), a clean fard, a site plan. Attempt a written family scheme with a lawyer at the table — a registered partition deed among consenting co-sharers accomplishes in a month what suits accomplish in years. Failing that, choose the right forum and pursue it without gaps; partition proceedings die of inattention, not of difficulty. And meanwhile, disturb nothing unilaterally: in partition law, the patient co-sharer with documents finishes ahead of the aggressive one with possession.
