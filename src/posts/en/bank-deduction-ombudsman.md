Money leaves Pakistani bank accounts in ways their owners never authorised — ATM captures that debit anyway, "system reversal" promises that never reverse, online transfers made by fraudsters with suspicious ease. The banks' complaint counters absorb anger efficiently; what they respect is the machinery above them, and every account holder should know its name: the **Banking Mohtasib**.

## First, the internal complaint — properly

The route requires a first formal round with the bank itself: a **written complaint** (the bank's complaint form, its email channel, or a letter against acknowledgment — not a verbal visit) stating the transaction, the amount, the date, and what you demand. The bank gets the prescribed window — commonly **45 days** — to resolve. Two disciplines here: get and keep the **complaint number**, and put the facts precisely (transaction IDs, ATM location and time, the fraud call's number and timing). This record is the foundation of everything upstream.

For fraud in progress, the first call is same-day, to the bank's helpline, demanding **blocking of the account and disputed beneficiary** — recovery of fraud transfers is mostly a function of how fast the chain froze, as our cybercrime article details.

## The Banking Mohtasib

If the bank rejects, half-answers or ignores you, the **Banking Mohtasib Pakistan** — the statutory banking ombudsman — takes complaints against scheduled banks: unauthorized debits, failed ATM/POS/online transactions, fraudulent transfers where bank negligence or systems are implicated, loan and account service failures, and misconduct of bank staff. Filing is **free**, on a simple form with your documents and the bank's response attached, within the limitation window after the bank's final answer; no lawyer is required, hearings are informal, and orders against banks are effective under the framework, with a review structure above.

The Mohtasib's published decisions show consistent patterns worth knowing: banks are held to their **own security systems** — transactions that bypassed proper authentication, SIM-swap frauds enabled by telco-bank gaps, and delayed action after fraud reports recur as refund grounds; "customer shared the OTP" is the bank's strongest card, which is why the fraud-education paragraph in every article of this series repeats itself: **no legitimate caller ever needs your OTP**.

The State Bank's **Sunwai** portal now runs alongside as the front door for banking conduct complaints, routing matters into the same accountability plumbing — use it as the parallel track, with the Mohtasib as the adjudicative one.

## The recurring cases, and their playing

**ATM captured/short-paid cash:** report same day from the helpline, note the ATM ID; these turn on machine balancing records banks hold and the Mohtasib makes them produce. **Failed transfer, money gone:** the trail is electronic and complete; persistence retrieves it. **"Dormancy", charges and deduction creep:** schedule-of-charges disputes are won by comparing your statements to the bank's published schedule — banks refund quietly when asked formally. **Fraud transfers:** the battle is negligence allocation — your speed of report and the bank's authentication failures versus your disclosure of credentials; file with both the bank and the cybercrime machinery, because each record strengthens the other.

## The habits that make you hard to wrong

Real-time SMS/app alerts on every account; statements actually read monthly; disputes raised the day noticed (delay is the bank's friend at every forum); and every interaction converted to writing with a number. Banks triage complainants ruthlessly by persistence — the account holder with a complaint number, a paper trail and the Mohtasib's address visible in their correspondence sits in a different queue from the one who only shouted at the branch.
