Most defamation matters in Punjab today begin with a phone screen: an accusation in a community group, a "scam alert" post about a business, a family dispute narrated to eleven hundred followers. Both directions of this problem — being attacked and being accused — follow procedures that are now well worn.

## If you are the one defamed

**Hour one: preserve.** Before confrontation tempts the poster to delete-and-deny, capture the evidence: full-screen recordings scrolling through the post, its URL, the account profile, the share count, screenshots including date and time, and the identities of witnesses who saw it. For high-stakes matters, have the material notarised or preserved through formal mechanisms — authenticity challenges are the standard defence.

**Day one: report and notice.** Report the content through the platform's own process (false accusations frequently violate community standards, and platform takedown is the fastest removal available). In parallel, have counsel serve the **statutory notice** demanding deletion, retraction and apology within the legal window — the notice is a precondition of the tribunal claim and, served early, often ends the matter.

**The forums.** For compensation and binding retraction: the **defamation tribunal** under the Punjab Act, with its presumed damages. For anonymous accounts and content-focused outcomes: the **cybercrime route** under PECA, whose machinery can trace accounts and drive takedowns. The tracks can run together; which leads depends on whether your priority is money, removal, or unmasking.

**Anonymous attackers** are less anonymous than they believe: complaints through the cybercrime process can reach account data, and the pattern of a post's first sharers usually identifies its origin socially long before any forensic step.

## If you are the one accused

**Stop digging.** No follow-up posts, no "let them sue" stories, no deletion-with-denial (deletion is discoverable and reads as consciousness of guilt — preserve your own copy, then take the content down as a good-faith step, announced as such).

**Diagnose honestly with counsel.** Was what you posted *true and provable*? Truth remains a complete answer, but the burden is yours, and "everyone knows" is not evidence. Was it opinion on a matter of public interest, fairly grounded in facts? Fair comment protects opinions, not invented facts dressed as opinions. If neither: your best asset is speed — a retraction and apology inside the notice window, negotiated to close the matter, costs pride and little else; the same apology after a preliminary decree merely mitigates.

**Respond formally.** Every notice deserves a lawyer-drafted reply within its deadline — asserting defences where real, offering resolution where not. Silence converts into tribunal filings and, under the 2024 Act's early-decree mechanics, silence is now the most expensive strategy available.

## For businesses and pages

Pages that review, expose or comment as a format should operate with a standing discipline: verify before publishing, distinguish fact from opinion in the text itself, correct promptly and visibly when wrong, and archive their verification. That file — the pre-publication checks — is what a defence of responsible publication is built from.

The old internet instinct — post now, delete later if it gets hot — belongs to a legal era Punjab has closed. Treat every post about an identifiable person or business as a publication you may one day defend under oath, because that is precisely what it is.
