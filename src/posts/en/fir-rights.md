The phone call that an FIR has been registered is one of the most frightening moments in an ordinary person's life. Most of the damage people suffer afterwards comes not from the FIR itself but from panic — fleeing, confronting the complainant, or signing things at the police station. This article explains what an FIR is and what to do, calmly, in the first day.

## What an FIR is — and is not

A First Information Report under section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure is simply the recorded first version of an alleged cognizable offence. It is not a finding of guilt, not a court document, and not evidence of anything except that a complaint was made. Its contents can be — and very often are — exaggerated or false. But it does set the police machinery in motion, so it must be taken seriously.

## Your immediate rights

**The right to a copy.** You are entitled to a copy of the FIR. Get it immediately — everything a lawyer advises flows from what is actually written in it, which is often milder or wilder than what you have been told by phone.

**The right to know the offence.** The sections listed in the FIR determine whether the offence is bailable (release on surety is your right) or non-bailable (a court decides), and whether police can arrest without warrant.

**Protection against unnecessary arrest.** Arrest is not an automatic consequence of an FIR. For offences punishable below a certain threshold, the law and superior-court judgments require police to issue a notice to join the investigation rather than arrest first. Many arrests made in the first rush are avoidable with prompt legal intervention.

**The right to counsel.** You may consult and be represented by a lawyer from the very first moment — not just in court.

## What to do in the first 24 hours

1. **Get the FIR copy and send it to a lawyer.** A lawyer who has read the FIR can tell you the same day whether the offence is bailable, whether pre-arrest bail is needed, and which court to approach.
2. **If the offence is non-bailable, move for pre-arrest bail before anything else.** Interim protection can often be obtained within a day, and it removes the police's leverage entirely.
3. **Preserve your evidence now.** CCTV footage gets overwritten in days. Call records, location data, receipts, witnesses who saw where you were — collect them while they exist.
4. **Say nothing on the phone to the complainant's side.** Calls made in anger are recorded and produced in court. Threatening a complainant can itself become a fresh offence and destroys bail prospects.
5. **Do not sign anything at the police station** — not a "statement", not a "raazinama draft", not blank paper — without your lawyer present.

## What the police may and may not do

Police may investigate, summon you to join the investigation, and arrest where the law permits. They may not detain you beyond 24 hours without producing you before a Magistrate; may not torture or coerce a confession — a confession to police is inadmissible in evidence in any event; may not arrest women except in accordance with strict statutory conditions; and may not "settle" the case for money, which is a crime on their part.

## If the FIR is false

Pakistan's legal system is realistic about false FIRs — they are a recognised weapon in property, family and business disputes. The remedies are pre-arrest bail on the ground of mala fide, a petition to the High Court under section 561-A Cr.P.C. for quashing in clear cases, and prosecution of the complainant under section 182 Cr.P.C. or for perjury after acquittal. Which remedy fits depends entirely on the FIR's contents and timing — which is why the first step, always, is getting that copy to a lawyer within hours, not weeks.
