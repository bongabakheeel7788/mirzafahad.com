Many business owners in Pakistan run for years as a sole proprietorship, then wonder whether they should "become a company". The answer depends on what the business needs. This guide sets out the options and then walks through SECP registration.

## Sole proprietorship, partnership or company?

**Sole proprietorship.** No registration beyond an NTN from FBR and, if applicable, sales tax registration. Simplest and cheapest. The owner is personally liable for all business debts, and the business cannot easily take in investors or survive the owner.

**Partnership.** Registered with the Registrar of Firms under the Partnership Act, 1932, on the basis of a partnership deed. Suitable for two or more people working together. Partners are jointly and severally liable — each partner can be pursued for the whole of the firm's debts.

**Private limited company.** Registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) under the Companies Act, 2017. A separate legal person: it owns its property, signs its contracts and is liable for its debts. Shareholders' liability is limited to their shares. This is what most people mean by "a company".

A private limited company makes sense when the business needs to raise investment, bid for contracts that require it, protect the owners' personal assets from business risk, or be transferred or continued independently of any one person. It costs more to maintain — annual returns, audited accounts above certain thresholds, and a corporate tax return — so for a very small business it is often premature.

A **single-member company (SMC)** is a private limited company with one shareholder, available since the Companies Ordinance, 2002. It suits an individual who wants limited liability without a partner.

## The SECP registration process

Registration is now done online through SECP's eServices portal, and in ordinary cases takes a few working days.

1. **Choose and reserve a name.** The name must not be identical or similar to an existing company, must not contain prohibited words (such as those implying government patronage), and must end with "(Private) Limited" or "(SMC-Private) Limited". A name availability application is filed online; if approved, the name is reserved for a period during which incorporation must be completed.
2. **Prepare the constitutional documents.** The **Memorandum of Association** states the company's name, registered office province, principal line of business and share capital. The **Articles of Association** are the internal rules — directors, meetings, share transfers. SECP provides standard templates; for a company with more than one shareholder, the articles should be reviewed and adapted so that they reflect the actual agreement between the owners.
3. **Directors and subscribers.** A private company needs at least two directors (one for an SMC). Each provides CNIC, contact details and consent. Subscribers (initial shareholders) sign the memorandum.
4. **Registered office.** An address in Pakistan where notices can be served. It need not be owned; a rented office or, for a small company, a residential address is acceptable.
5. **File incorporation documents and pay the fee.** The fee depends on authorised share capital and whether filing is online. Documents are digitally signed through the portal.
6. **Certificate of incorporation.** SECP issues the certificate, and the company exists from that date. The company's NTN is now generated automatically in coordination with FBR.

## After incorporation

- **Bank account.** Open in the company's name with the certificate, memorandum, articles and a board resolution.
- **Sales tax registration**, if the business supplies taxable goods or services above the threshold.
- **Statutory registers and minutes.** Keep a register of members, directors and a minute book. These are inspected if there is ever a dispute between shareholders.
- **Annual filings.** Form A (annual return) and, where required, audited accounts, are filed with SECP each year. Late filing attracts penalties that accumulate.
- **Shareholders' agreement.** Where there is more than one owner, a separate shareholders' agreement dealing with contributions, roles, salaries, exit and deadlock is strongly advisable. The articles alone rarely cover what happens when partners fall out — and that is when the document is needed.

## Common mistakes

- Registering a company and then continuing to invoice and receive payments as a sole proprietor. The company then has no business, and the owner has no limited liability.
- Using the standard articles for a two-partner company without thinking about what happens if one wants to leave.
- Ignoring annual returns for several years. Reviving a company with accumulated defaults costs more than the filings would have.

## Trademarks

Company registration does **not** protect the brand. A company name and a trademark are different rights. A business that trades under a name or logo should apply to register it with the Intellectual Property Organisation of Pakistan (IPO), a separate process with its own timeline.

If you are deciding whether to incorporate, the most useful information to bring to a consultation is a brief description of the business, who the owners are, and what you expect to happen in the next two or three years. The right structure follows from that.
