Girvi is everywhere in rural Punjab: land handed over against a loan, the lender cultivating it for years, the owner hoping someday to redeem. The arrangement is ancient, legal, and constantly litigated — because the parties rarely write down what the law has been precise about since 1882.

## What a mortgage is

Under section 58 of the **Transfer of Property Act 1882**, a mortgage is the transfer of an interest in specific immovable property to secure a loan or obligation. The borrower is the **mortgagor**, the lender the **mortgagee**. Crucially, a mortgage transfers an *interest*, never full ownership: however long it lasts, behind every mortgage stands the owner's right to pay and take the property back.

## The types you will actually meet

- **Usufructuary mortgage** — the classic **girvi**. Possession is delivered to the lender, who takes the rents and produce in lieu of interest (or toward the principal, as agreed). There is no personal promise to pay by a fixed date and no right in the lender to sell; the relationship ends when the owner redeems.
- **Simple mortgage** — the owner keeps possession and personally undertakes to pay; on default, the lender's remedy is through court sale of the property, not self-help.
- **Mortgage by conditional sale** — dressed as a sale that becomes absolute on default. Courts examine the substance of these carefully, because moneylenders have long used "sale" paperwork to disguise what is really a security transaction; the distinction decides whether the borrower can redeem.
- **Mortgage by deposit of title deeds** — the banking form: the title documents are deposited with the bank as security. This is what sits behind most urban house-finance files.

## Form and registration

Beyond a nominal principal amount, a mortgage of immovable property (other than by deposit of title deeds) must be effected by a **registered instrument**, signed and attested. The village reality — a girvi written on a stamp paper before two witnesses, or purely oral with possession delivered — falls short of this. Such arrangements are not void nothings: courts regularly work out the parties' rights, and possession plus payment evidence counts. But every element becomes contestable — the amount, the terms, whether it was girvi or sale, whether produce counted against principal. A registered mortgage deed costs little against the litigation an unwritten girvi breeds. A mutation of the mortgage in the revenue record protects both sides further.

## Redemption: the owner's protected right

The right to redeem (section 60 of the Act) is the heart of mortgage law. Once the secured amount is paid or tendered, the owner is entitled to possession and to re-transfer of the mortgaged interest. Two principles protect the villager:

- **Once a mortgage, always a mortgage.** Clauses that purport to kill the right of redemption — "if not repaid in five years the land becomes the lender's" — are treated as clogs on redemption and are not enforced according to their threat.
- **Long possession by the mortgagee does not make him owner.** His possession is as mortgagee; it is not adverse, and decades of cultivation do not convert girvi into ownership. The limitation period for a redemption suit is long, but it is not infinite — old family girvis should be redeemed, not left to another generation.

Procedurally, redemption is enforced by a **suit for redemption and possession** in civil court. Punjab has also long provided a **summary route under the Redemption of Mortgages (Punjab) Act 1913**, allowing petitions to the revenue collector for redemption of smaller agricultural mortgages — faster and cheaper where it applies, with the civil court available if the summary route fails or is contested.

## Accounting disputes

The second great girvi fight is arithmetic: the owner says twenty years of crops repaid the loan threefold; the lender says expenses consumed it. The agreed terms control where they exist; where they do not, courts settle accounts on the evidence. This is another argument for writing the deal down — specify whether produce goes against interest, principal, or neither, and whether the lender may make improvements and charge for them.

## Practical rules for both sides

- Write and register the mortgage; record it by mutation.
- State the principal, the possession terms, and how produce or rent is credited.
- Owners: keep every payment receipt and redeem within your generation.
- Lenders: never rely on a disguised "sale" — substance prevails over labels.

Whether you are trying to redeem land your father girvied decades ago or to secure a loan you are extending, a brief consultation with the girvi writing (if any), the fard and the payment record will settle the right route — summary or civil — and what the realistic account looks like.
