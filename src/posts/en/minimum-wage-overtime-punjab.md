The minimum wage notification is announced every year with the budget, reported in every newspaper — and ignored on a vast scale in workshops, power looms, shops and small factories across Punjab. Workers often assume the notified wage is a suggestion, or that claiming it means losing the job with nothing to show. In fact the wage is a statutory floor, overtime at double rate is a statutory right, and the recovery forums are cheaper and faster than most people expect.

## How the minimum wage is fixed

Under Punjab's minimum wage legislation — the current consolidated statute being the **Punjab Minimum Wages Act 2019**, successor to the 1961 Ordinance regime — a **Minimum Wages Board** recommends rates, and the Punjab government notifies them. In practice the headline rate for unskilled workers is revised with the annual budget, with separate schedules for skilled and semi-skilled categories and for certain industries.

Three features matter legally:

- The notified wage is a **floor, not a package**: it is the minimum for the unskilled grade, and skilled categories carry higher notified rates.
- It binds **private employers across covered employments** — factories, shops, commercial establishments, including the small units that dominate Faisalabad's loom and garments economy. Coverage questions arise at the margins (pure domestic arrangements, some agricultural work), but the ordinary shop or factory worker is covered.
- Paying less is an **offence**, not merely a civil shortfall. The arrears remain recoverable by the worker regardless.

Never rely on a remembered figure: the number changes, and the current notification is the document a court will apply.

## Overtime: the double-rate rule

Working hours are capped by the **Factories Act 1934** for factories and the **Punjab Shops and Establishments Ordinance 1969** for shops and commercial establishments — broadly the familiar pattern of an eight-to-nine hour day and a forty-eight hour week, with rest intervals and a weekly holiday. Work beyond the statutory limits is **overtime, payable at twice the ordinary rate of pay**.

The arithmetic employers avoid: a worker on monthly wages has an hourly rate (derivable from the monthly wage), and each overtime hour costs the employer double that. "We pay a fixed amount for late sitting" or overtime at single rate is unlawful underpayment. Keep your own record — duty timings, gate passes, duty rosters, even daily SMS or WhatsApp instructions to stay late — because the employer's registers, which the law requires it to maintain, have a way of showing no overtime at all.

## Deductions the employer cannot make

The **Payment of Wages Act 1936** allows only listed deductions: fines within strict limits and procedure, deductions for absence, for damage or loss caused by the worker's neglect (with an opportunity to explain), house accommodation, advances, and similar enumerated heads. Outside the list, deductions are unlawful — which condemns several common practices:

- **"Security" withheld from wages** and never returned.
- **Fines without the required procedure** or exceeding the permitted proportion of wages.
- **Collective punishment deductions** for stock shortages no one traces to the worker.
- **Delayed wages** — the Act fixes wage periods and payment deadlines; chronic late payment is itself actionable.

## Filing a wage claim

The forum for unpaid or delayed wages, unlawful deductions and overtime is the **Authority under the Payment of Wages Act** (and the claim machinery under the minimum wage law for sub-minimum payment); workmen can also route disputes through the Labour Court under the Punjab Industrial Relations Act 2010. Practical notes:

1. **Claims are cheap and summary** — designed for workers, not for stamp-duty battles.
2. **Compensation can be added** to the wages ordered, which gives the employer a reason to settle early.
3. **Limitation is short** — wage claims must be brought promptly, though delay can be condoned for sufficient cause. Do not accumulate years of underpayment before acting.
4. **Group claims work.** Ten underpaid workers filing together change the negotiation completely and protect each other against retaliation, which — if it comes as a termination — is itself challengeable through the grievance route.

Keep the paper: appointment letter if any, salary slips, bank or JazzCash/Easypaisa credits, attendance proof, and the current minimum wage notification. Where pay was always in unrecorded cash, colleagues' testimony and the employer's own registers (which the forum can summon) fill the gap.

Wage cases are usually decided by one comparison — what the notification commands against what the record shows you received. A brief consultation with whatever pay proof you have will put a rupee figure on the claim and pick the right forum before limitation becomes the employer's best defence.
