School fees are one of the most regulated and most violated areas of consumer life in Punjab. Parents assume the school's fee card is law; in fact, private schools sit under a statutory registration and regulation regime with real rules on what they may charge — and real forums that hear parents.

## The regulatory frame

Private schools in Punjab operate under the province's private educational institutions framework — registration is mandatory, and with it comes the regulator's jurisdiction over fees. The core disciplines: fees must follow the **structure disclosed at registration and admission**; **annual increases are capped** by the framework and its notifications, with anything beyond requiring justification to and approval from the authority; and schools must not convert compulsory charges into disguised fee raises through "annual charges", "development funds" and similar line items — a practice the regulator and courts have repeatedly confronted.

The **summer vacation fee** deserves its own line: it has been the subject of successive government directions and court proceedings over the years, with relief ordered against full vacation billing by higher-fee schools at various points. The applicable position in any given year turns on the current notifications — but the categorical claim schools make, that vacations are billable like term months as of right, has not survived scrutiny whenever tested.

## What schools cannot lawfully do

The recurring abuses have recurring answers. **Mid-year fee revisions** outside the disclosed structure are challengeable. **Compulsory purchases** — uniforms, books, stationery tied to the school's own shop at the school's own prices — are a regulated practice, and bundling them into fee coercion compounds the violation. **Withholding results, transcripts or school-leaving certificates** to enforce disputed charges is the classic pressure tactic, and both the regulator and the constitutional courts have treated children's education and records as the wrong hostage: retaliation against a student for the parents' bona fide dispute is the strongest complaint a parent can file. **Expulsion over a fee dispute** while the dispute is properly raised sits in the same category.

None of this means fees are optional: undisputed fees must be paid, and a parent who simply stops paying hands the school the high ground. The protected position is paying the undisputed portion while formally contesting the excess.

## The complaint ladder

Start with the school in **writing** — a dated letter or email to the principal stating the specific violation (the increase percentage, the notification it exceeds, the withheld document) and the remedy sought. Escalate to the **registering authority** for private schools in your district — the education authorities' complaint channels — attaching the fee cards, receipts, and correspondence; the regulator can direct compliance and, at the extreme, act against registration. Parallel routes exist through the province's citizen-complaint portals, and parents' associations filing collectively have historically moved schools that ignored individuals. Where a school retaliates against the child, or the dispute involves recovery of overcharged amounts, the courts — including the consumer forum for service deficiencies and the constitutional jurisdiction against regulatory inaction — complete the ladder.

## Practical counsel for parents

Keep every fee card, receipt and circular from admission onward; the admission-time fee structure is your baseline document. Dispute in writing, pay the undisputed, and never negotiate through the child. And act with other parents where the issue is school-wide — fee regulation is one of the few areas where collective complaints have repeatedly and visibly worked in Punjab.
