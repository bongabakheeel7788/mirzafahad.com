"Court marriage" is one of the most searched legal phrases in Pakistan, and one of the most misunderstood. There is no marriage performed *by a court*. What the phrase describes is an ordinary Islamic nikah, contracted by two consenting adults — usually without family involvement — documented carefully, often with a lawyer arranging the formalities. Its legal foundation is simple and absolute: **a sui juris adult woman can marry of her own free will. Parental permission is not a legal requirement.** The superior courts have affirmed this repeatedly.

## What actually happens

The couple engages a lawyer; identities and ages are verified from CNICs (both parties must be adults — eighteen for men, and per current Punjab law sixteen for women, though CNIC-age adults are the practical norm and under-18 marriages carry legal risk under child-marriage laws); a nikah is performed by a nikah khwan before two adult male witnesses (or as Islamic law prescribes), with offer, acceptance and dower recorded; the **nikahnama is filled completely and signed**; and the marriage is **registered** with the Union Council concerned, producing the NADRA marriage registration certificate. An affidavit of free will from the bride, sometimes supported by her statement recorded before a magistrate or in a protective petition, is standard practice where family opposition is feared.

Every column of the nikahnama matters. Column 18 (delegation of the right of divorce), the dower columns, and conditions agreed between the parties should be filled deliberately, not left to the nikah khwan's habit of drawing lines through them.

## Validity

A marriage contracted this way is as valid as any arranged marriage — religiously and legally. Non-registration does not invalidate the nikah itself (registration is a legal duty with penalties, not a condition of validity), but an unregistered marriage invites practical misery: NADRA records, spousal visas, inheritance and legitimacy questions all lean on the registration. Register immediately, always.

## The myths

**"The girl's wali must consent."** As a matter of Pakistani law, no. An adult woman's marriage without her guardian's consent is valid — this has been settled at the highest level for decades.

**"The family can get it cancelled."** There is no legal process by which parents "cancel" an adult child's valid marriage. What families sometimes do instead is file abduction FIRs — which brings us to safety.

**"Court marriage means the couple is safe."** The paper protects the marriage; it does not by itself protect the people. Where threats exist, the practical toolkit is: the bride's statement recorded under section 164 Cr.P.C. (defeating abduction FIRs at the root), a **protection petition** before the High Court seeking directions that police not harass the couple, and shelter arrangements where needed. These should be set in motion at once, not after the first FIR.

## Documents to keep

The original nikahnama, the Union Council registration certificate, the NADRA marriage certificate, the free-will affidavit, and copies of both CNICs — kept in more than one place. In any later dispute (family criminal complaints, visa processes, inheritance), this file is the marriage.

## A word to families

The law's position does not change with anger. FIRs filed to undo an adult daughter's marriage fail, but not before causing arrests, bail applications and permanent estrangement. Families who fear an unsuitable match achieve more through engagement than through the police station — the legal system, in these cases, is firmly on the couple's side.
