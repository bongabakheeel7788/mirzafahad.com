The parcel arrives and the branded phone is a plastic dummy; or the "cash on delivery" box was paid for and opened only after the rider left; or the payment went through and the seller's page vanished. Online shopping disputes in Pakistan feel lawless, but they are not — the law gives you three distinct routes, and choosing the right one at the start saves months.

## First, sort the problem into its legal box

**A genuine seller, a bad product.** The shop exists, delivers, answers messages — but the product is defective, counterfeit or not what was advertised. This is a **consumer law** problem.

**A dishonest scheme.** A page built to take payments and disappear, a seller impersonating a known brand, repeated "re-booking fees" squeezed from you. Dishonesty from the outset makes it **criminal fraud**, not a consumer dispute.

**A payment problem.** You were charged twice, or paid by card to a merchant who never shipped. Your **bank** has a dispute mechanism of its own.

The boxes overlap, and you can pursue more than one, but each has its own forum, deadline and style of evidence.

## The consumer court route

The Punjab Consumer Protection Act 2005 applies to products and services bought for personal use, and nothing in it exempts online sellers. The mechanics:

1. **Serve a written notice** on the seller — the Act requires a 15-day notice giving them a chance to repair, replace or refund before you sue. Send it to the address the business shows, by courier with proof, and keep a copy.
2. **File a claim** before the District Consumer Court if the notice brings nothing. Faisalabad has one; claims are drafted simply, court fee is nominal, and you may appear in person, though drafting matters more than people expect.
3. **Relief** can include replacement, refund, damages for loss caused, and the Act carries penal consequences for sellers who ignore orders.

Act quickly: the Act contains short limitation windows that run from when the defect or default surfaced, so a claim parked for months can die on timing alone. The practical weakness of this route is identification — a consumer court needs a defendant with a name and address, which is exactly what fly-by-night pages hide.

## The FIA route for actual fraud

Where deception was the business model, the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 applies — electronic fraud and related offences cover inducing a person to part with money through dishonest misrepresentation made via an information system. The complaint goes to the **FIA Cybercrime Wing**: online through its complaint portal, or in writing to the circle office (Faisalabad has one). Attach your full evidence set. The FIA can trace the account that received your money, the SIM and the page — the things you cannot.

Parallel to PECA, plain cheating under **section 420 PPC** remains available through the local police where the fraudster is identifiable. For small amounts, be realistic: the FIA prioritises patterns and larger frauds, which is why your complaint matters even when recovery is uncertain — it builds the pattern that gets a repeat fraudster caught.

## The payment-side levers

If you paid by **debit or credit card**, ask your bank in writing for a transaction dispute or chargeback for goods not delivered, as soon as the problem is clear — card scheme windows are limited. For **bank transfers and wallet payments**, immediately report to your own bank and the receiving institution's helpline that the account is receiving fraud proceeds; a fast report sometimes freezes funds before withdrawal. Cash on delivery has no payment lever, which is why the only safe COD habit is **opening the parcel before paying or on camera**.

Marketplace platforms with escrow and official refund policies sit between these routes — use the platform complaint first, because it is the fastest refund mechanism that exists, and escalate to the legal routes when it fails.

## Evidence habits that decide these cases

Screenshot the listing **before** ordering, not after it is edited. Film the unboxing of any valuable COD or prepaid parcel in one unbroken video showing the sealed packaging and courier label. Keep chats unexported and intact on the device, since forensic value lives in the original. These habits cost nothing and convert a hopeless complaint into a provable one.

Which route fits — notice and consumer claim, FIA complaint, chargeback, or two of them in parallel — depends on the seller, the amount and the paper you hold. A brief consultation with your screenshots and payment record usually settles the strategy in one sitting.
