A loom worker's hand is crushed; the employer pays the first hospital bill as "help" and considers the matter closed. Under the law it is nowhere near closed. If the establishment is covered by Punjab's social security scheme, that worker is entitled to free treatment, cash benefits during recovery, and a pension if the hand never works again — all from an institution funded by contributions the employer was supposed to be paying all along. PESSI is the most under-claimed entitlement in Punjab's industrial economy.

## The scheme and who it covers

The **Provincial Employees' Social Security Ordinance 1965** created what is now the **Punjab Employees Social Security Institution (PESSI)**. It applies to industrial and commercial establishments notified under the scheme — textile units, factories, shops and commercial concerns above modest size thresholds — and protects **secured workers**: employees up to a wage ceiling that the government revises, which in practice has tracked the minimum-wage bands so that the ordinary industrial workforce stays covered.

The contribution is a **percentage of wages paid entirely by the employer** — historically six percent — with **no deduction from the worker permitted**. An employer who docks "social security" from a pay packet is violating the Ordinance twice over.

## What a secured worker gets

The package is wider than most workers, and many employers, realise:

- **Medical care** — free treatment for the secured worker **and dependants** at social security hospitals, dispensaries and contracted facilities, including medicines, specialist care, and in defined serious cases referral for advanced treatment.
- **Sickness benefit** — a cash allowance for certified sickness absence, paid at a substantial proportion of wages for up to the statutory number of days in a year, higher and longer for certain serious illnesses such as tuberculosis and cancer.
- **Injury benefit** — for employment injuries, cash benefit at a higher proportion of wages during treatment, with **no contribution-history condition**: an injury on the first day of covered work qualifies.
- **Disablement pension and gratuity** — where a work injury leaves permanent disability, a pension scaled to the loss of earning capacity, or a lump sum for smaller permanent losses.
- **Maternity benefit** — paid leave benefit for secured women, with pre- and post-natal medical care.
- **Death grant and survivors' pension** — funeral grant and pensions for the widow and children where death results from employment injury, and an *iddat* benefit besides.

Benefit rates and ceilings are adjusted over time; treat the proportions above as the mechanism and confirm current figures from PESSI.

## Registration: the employer's duty, the worker's problem

The employer must register the establishment, register each secured worker, and pay monthly contributions. The chronic Faisalabad pattern is partial compliance: the establishment registered, but half the workforce kept off the rolls, or shown at lower wages.

What a worker should do:

1. **Ask for your social security card / registration number.** Its absence is itself the warning.
2. **Keep employment proof** — the same file this series keeps recommending: appointment letter, slips, attendance, bank credits.
3. **On injury, go to the social security hospital and name the employer.** PESSI's machinery can assess an evading employer retrospectively, and an employment injury claim does not die because the employer defaulted; the institution recovers from the employer. Courts have little sympathy for employers who pocket the contribution advantage and then plead non-registration.

## Claiming, and when PESSI refuses

Claims are lodged at the PESSI directorate or dispensary serving the establishment's area, with the card or registration particulars, CNIC, and the medical certification the scheme's own facilities issue. Disputes — refusal of benefit, wage-ceiling arguments, whether an accident was an employment injury — go before the **Social Security Court**, a dedicated forum under the Ordinance, with appeal lying onward. Time limits apply, so lodge claims promptly and in writing.

Two boundary notes. The scheme covers the employment injury field that older compensation law (the Workmen's Compensation regime) covers for non-secured workers — which law applies depends on coverage, and an injured worker outside PESSI is not without remedy. And PESSI benefits sit alongside, not instead of, EOBI's pension scheme: the two institutions, contributions and benefits are separate, and a compliant employer owes both.

If you or a family member has an unpaid injury claim, a refused card, or an employer deducting contributions it should be paying, the documents — pay record, medical papers, any PESSI correspondence — usually make the position obvious quickly. A brief consultation with that file settles whether the next letter goes to the employer, the Institution, or the Social Security Court.
