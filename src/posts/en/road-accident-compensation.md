Road deaths and injuries produce two legal events in Pakistan, and most families pursue only one. The criminal case against the driver proceeds — often to a raazinama — while the **civil compensation claim**, frequently worth far more to a bereaved household, is never filed because nobody told the family it existed.

## The three compensation streams

**The Fatal Accidents Act claim.** Where negligent driving kills, the dependents — spouse, children, parents — may sue the **driver and the vehicle's owner** for the pecuniary loss the death caused: the deceased's earnings and support, computed over the years of dependency. Injury cases run on parallel principles for treatment costs, lost earnings and disability. These are civil suits, independent of the criminal case's fate, and a compromise in the criminal case does not automatically extinguish them unless the settlement genuinely covered civil claims — a drafting point that matters enormously on both sides of any raazinama.

**Motor insurance.** The law mandates third-party insurance precisely so that accident victims recover against a fund rather than a driver's empty pockets; the insurer stands behind the owner's liability and is joined in the claim. The insurance certificate's particulars enter the file at the FIR stage — one of several reasons the FIR is not a formality.

**Compensation within the criminal process.** Courts convicting for rash and negligent driving can order compensation, and the qisas/diyat framework applies to the deaths and injuries themselves, with diyat's annually notified value setting reference points that also discipline settlement talks.

## The first 48 hours decide the claim

Everything the eventual claim needs is perishable. The **FIR** anchors the event: insist it records the vehicle's registration number, the driver's identity, and the accident's mechanics accurately — an FIR written as "unknown vehicle" because the family was at the hospital is the single most common claim-killer, and prompt supplementary statements can still cure it. **Photographs** of vehicles, skid marks and the scene; **witness names with phone numbers** before the crowd disperses; the **medico-legal record** initiated at the hospital; and thereafter every prescription, bill and travel receipt — the arithmetic of compensation is receipts.

For the deceased's dependency computation: employment records, salary slips, business accounts, even testimony of trade earnings — the claim quantifies a life's support, and evidence of earnings is its spine.

## Realism about the process

These suits take time, and defendants' insurers contest quantum hard; but decrees are real, executable against owner and insurer, and the claims' existence changes settlement mathematics in the criminal case — a family negotiating a raazinama while a quantified civil claim stands behind them negotiates from a different chair entirely. Conversely, families should sign nothing releasing "all claims" for the criminal compromise amount without understanding exactly what they are surrendering.

## For vehicle owners

The mirror lesson: your liability follows your vehicle, including with a driver at the wheel. Keep third-party insurance genuinely current — the premium is trivial against an uninsured fatal claim — and understand that lending the car lends your liability with it.

Grief makes families accept whatever the first fortnight offers. The law offers considerably more — to those whose file was built while the evidence still existed.
