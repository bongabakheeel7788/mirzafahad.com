A SIM you never bought can exist in your name right now — issued years ago on a photocopy of your CNIC, activated by a franchise agent gaming sales targets, or verified through a lifted fingerprint. Until it surfaces in a harassment complaint or a criminal investigation, you will not know. Five minutes of checking, and a documented blocking routine, close a risk that costs nothing to close and plenty to ignore.

## How SIMs end up in your name

Before biometric verification became mandatory, SIMs were sold against CNIC photocopies — copies you handed to schools, banks, hotels and employers. Many of those SIMs are still alive. Even after the biometric verification system, cases emerged of franchise staff activating extra SIMs during a legitimate visit — you verify your thumb once, multiple activations ride on it. PTA caps the number of SIMs one CNIC can hold per operator precisely because registrations multiply quietly; the cap and its enforcement mechanics change, so check the current limit rather than assume it.

## Step one: see the list

SMS your **CNIC number (no dashes) to 668**. The reply shows the count of SIMs registered against your CNIC with each operator. PTA also runs an online SIM information portal showing the same data. Two habits make this useful: check **all family members' CNICs**, especially elderly parents whose copies circulated for decades, and recheck **once or twice a year** — this is a recurring hygiene task, not a one-time event.

The 668 check shows counts and partial numbers, not full numbers — enough to spot that the count is wrong. If you hold two Jazz SIMs and the system shows four, two strangers are walking around as you.

## Step two: block what is not yours

For each unknown SIM:

1. Go to the **operator's own franchise or customer service centre** with your original CNIC.
2. After biometric verification, ask for the unknown SIM to be **blocked and de-registered** from your CNIC.
3. Insist on a **written or SMS acknowledgement** — the date you disowned the SIM is the date your responsibility realistically ends, so evidence of that date is the whole point.

If the franchise stalls, or the operator claims the SIM "cannot" be removed, escalate to **PTA's complaint management system** — online, via the PTA app, or in writing. PTA directs operators on exactly these complaints, and an unresolved operator complaint is a precondition PTA expects, so do the franchise step first and keep its proof.

## Why this matters: the liability picture

Every SIM traces to its registered owner. If an unknown SIM in your name is used for fraud calls, threats, ransom demands or terrorism financing, the first door the investigation knocks on is yours. You are not automatically guilty — actual use and knowledge matter, and the real user's device identity, location pattern and voice are all traceable — but you inherit the burden of the explanation, possibly after a night at a police station. A documented prior block converts "accused" into "complainant": you reported the registration as fraudulent before anything happened, on the record.

There is also a civil tail: SIMs are used to register mobile wallets, and wallets in your name can carry transactions you never made — a problem this series covers separately under mobile wallet fraud. Cleaning the SIM layer protects the layers built on it.

## If misuse has already happened

If police or FIA contact you about a SIM you never obtained: cooperate, state immediately and in writing that the SIM was never yours, produce your 668 records and any blocking history, and get counsel involved early — the goal is to be classified as a victim of identity misuse, which you are. Simultaneously complain to PTA and the operator, because the franchise that activated the SIM has records (date, location, agent) that help establish the fraud, and operators move faster when a criminal case is attached.

If someone used your CNIC copy to obtain the SIM, that is personation and cheating under the PPC, and in online contexts PECA 2016 offences may attach — a complaint of your own builds the record that you are the injured party.

## The routine worth keeping

Check 668 for the household twice a year, block strangers the week you find them, and keep the acknowledgements in the same folder as your CNIC copies. Where a blocked SIM resurfaces, or an investigation has already touched your name, the sequence of complaints and their timing matters — a short consultation with your 668 record and any police or PTA correspondence settles what to file and where.
