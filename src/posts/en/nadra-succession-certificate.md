Until 2021, releasing a deceased person's bank account or shares meant a court case that could take months. The Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act, 2021 changed that: for straightforward cases, NADRA now issues succession certificates through its offices, often within weeks. This article explains how it works and when the old court route still applies.

## What the certificate is for

A **succession certificate** covers movable assets — bank accounts, savings certificates, shares, insurance proceeds, provident fund. A **letter of administration** covers immovable property. NADRA issues both for eligible cases. Banks and companies will not release a deceased customer's assets to anyone, including a spouse, without one.

## Who can apply

Any legal heir of a deceased Muslim or non-Muslim citizen can apply, provided the death occurred and the heirs are not in dispute. That last part matters: the NADRA route is built for **uncontested** cases. If any heir objects, or the list of heirs is itself disputed, NADRA will not proceed and the matter goes to court.

## The process, step by step

1. **Apply** at a NADRA Succession Facilitation Unit (or online through NADRA's portal) with the deceased's CNIC and death certificate (from the Union Council).
2. **Family Registration Certificate.** NADRA generates the deceased's family tree from its own database. This is why the CNIC family records matter — an heir missing from NADRA's records (for example, a child whose B-form was never made) complicates the application.
3. **Details of assets.** You list the bank accounts, certificates or property for which the certificate is sought — with account numbers and branch details.
4. **Public notice.** A notice is published inviting objections, with a 14-day window.
5. **Biometric verification of all heirs.** Every adult legal heir must appear and verify by thumbprint that they consent. Heirs abroad can verify at Pakistani embassies or through NADRA's overseas arrangements.
6. **Issuance.** If no objection is received, the certificate issues — commonly within two to four weeks of completed biometrics.

## When you still need the court

The court route under the Succession Act remains necessary where any heir refuses to verify or objects; where there is a dispute about who the heirs are (a second marriage, a disputed adoption, an heir missing from records); where a minor's share requires guardianship arrangements the family cannot agree on; where the deceased was a foreign national; or where an asset-holder insists on a court certificate. In practice, disputes among heirs are the most frequent reason.

The court process is a petition before the Civil Judge, notice to heirs and general public, evidence of relationship, and a certificate on furnishing a surety bond. A contested case takes several months; an uncontested one, six to ten weeks in most districts.

## Practical points

- Do the **inheritance mutation for land separately** — a succession certificate for bank accounts does not by itself transfer agricultural land; the revenue office enters an inheritance mutation on the basis of the heirship record.
- **Never leave out an heir** to speed things up. A certificate obtained by concealing an heir is challengeable, and every payment made under it can be reopened.
- **Women's biometric consent is consent for the certificate, not surrender of their share.** Some families obtain a certificate listing all heirs and then pressure sisters to "release" their portion. A release deed signed without consideration and without independent advice is contestable.
- Keep multiple certified copies; every bank branch will want to see an original.

If your family's case is clean — heirs agreed, records complete — you may not need a lawyer at all for the NADRA route. Where anything is contested, get advice before applying, because the forum you start in shapes the fight.
