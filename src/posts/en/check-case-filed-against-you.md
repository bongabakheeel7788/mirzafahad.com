"Tumhare khilaf case kar diya hai" — the threat arrives in property disputes, business fallouts and family wars, and then nothing: no summons, no police visit, only ambient dread. Meanwhile the opposite catastrophe also runs daily: people discover at the airport, or at a routine police check, that a case has been quietly proceeding — sometimes to ex parte decree or non-bailable warrant — at an address they left years ago. Both problems have the same cure: knowing how to check.

## Checking court cases

Punjab's judiciary has put case information substantially online. The **district judiciary case-search systems** and the **Lahore High Court's** search facilities allow lookups by party name and CNIC across pending matters, with case numbers, next dates and stage; **cause lists** publish daily. A methodical check covers: the districts where you live and work, where the potential opponent lives, and where any disputed property or transaction sits — since our forum articles explain why each of those courts might host a case. Name-search limitations are real (spelling variants, common names), which is why the CNIC-based searches and a lawyer's clerk's confirmation at the relevant court's registry remain the completing steps for anything serious.

The high-stakes version — you suspect an **ex parte proceeding** at an old address — deserves urgency: decrees passed without real service can be set aside, but the applications carry their own limitation clocks from knowledge, and our limitation article explains how little mercy those clocks show.

## Checking FIRs and police matters

FIR visibility is patchier by design, but the practical routes work: **counsel's inquiry** at the station concerned (the everyday method — a lawyer confirms registration and obtains the copy you are entitled to); the police's **online FIR and verification channels**, which in Punjab have grown to include FIR-status and character-certificate facilities (the police character certificate itself is the formal document proving your record clean — needed for jobs and visas, and obtainable online); and, where an FIR's existence is confirmed, the immediate strategy conversation our FIR-rights article maps — because the window between registration and arrest attempt is when pre-arrest bail does its work.

Warrant and proclamation status — whether a case has escalated to non-bailable warrants or absconder proceedings — shows in the court file and the case-search systems' stages; discovering it late is exactly the disaster the periodic check prevents, since proclamation machinery can grind toward property attachment while you know nothing.

## Who should check, and when

Make it routine if you are: in active dispute with anyone who threatens litigation; a guarantor or open-letter vehicle seller (our respective articles explain the liabilities that ripen silently); an overseas Pakistani with property under relatives' management (a departure-area discovery is the classic horror version); or changing address — with the corollary discipline of updating addresses with banks, NADRA and counterparties so service reaches you.

## On discovery

Finding something changes the task from intelligence to response: obtain the complete file (FIR copy, plaint, order sheets) through counsel before reacting; calendar every date and limitation; move for the protective step the matter demands — bail, appearance, setting aside — and never test the absconding route, which converts defendable cases into fugitive status with its own cascading machinery. The person who checks quarterly and responds within days holds every advantage the system offers; the person who "didn't want to know" meets the system at its least merciful point.
